## §1 — English subject rules (REF-03 C3 slice)

### 1.1 The communicative spine (Must-Cover part A)
English's spine is **flatter** than Bangla's tall decoding ladder — several High-load strands run in parallel. At C3 the **dominant strand is reading comprehension** (`5.1.1`/`5.1.2`, by far the most-cited across the 104 sessions — the spine of the whole class). Running alongside: **reading-aloud of numbers & sentences** (`4.1.x`/`4.2.x`), **guided writing** (`7.4.1`/`7.4.2`, U2/U5/U6), **explicit middle-sound phonics** (`1.1.2`/`3.1.2`, U4 — *new in Era B*), **ordinal numbers** (`7.2.3`, U4L6 — new), and **speaking/conversation** (`3.x`, woven). **Grammar is NOT named at C3** — it is taught implicitly (rule E-2: named only from C4). Conventions surface late (capitals `9.1.1`, comma/exclamation `9.1.2/3`, nouns/verbs/tenses `9.3.x`/`9.5.x` at U5/U6/U8) but as correctness-in-use, not as labelled grammar in the objective.

**Rule E-2 (named-from-C4).** Grammar/tense/Wh-questions are taught *implicitly* C1–C3 and *named* from C4. Take the objective from the lesson's **TG-validated learning-outcome code** (§5 / §5.1), never from the de-labelled pupil book.

**Rule E-1 (phonics handoff).** C3 extends phonics to **middle sounds** (U4, `1.1.2`/`3.1.2`) — it assumes C2's systematic initial/final sounds are mastered. Phonics is genuinely taught at C3 (not teacher-side only — that's C5).

### 1.2 Required vocabulary (Must-Cover part B)
C3 vocabulary is **distributed and in-context** (`8.1.1` recognise / `8.1.2` use familiar words; `TOP-ENG-C3-03`) — there is no single controlled glossary. The required-word list for a lesson = its **theme vocabulary** (greetings, family, numbers 1–100, commands, phonics target words, school/village/city life, cleanliness, water, fable). **Recommendation (live):** keep a **running local word-bank** across the C3 lessons (the strand never fully stops). Replacement/example content reuses theme words wherever the source allows. **F-01 holds on every word/example** — no Western names/foods/clothing; the C3 name surface is in §2 (3 Western + 6 non-Muslim-Bengali names, all ruled replace).

### 1.3 Bloom band (C3 — Class 3–5 band, from the Core pocket)
Indicative **chapter-scope** mix (climbs across a multi-period lesson): Remember 20–30% · Understand 25–35% · Apply 25–35% · Analyze 10–20% · Evaluate 0–10% · Create 0–10%. C3 dominant levels = **Understand · Apply**, with Analyze opening up in comprehension and fable work. **Comprehension rule:** reading-comprehension is High-load and easy to drill flat — set §2.3 so comprehension lessons reach **Understand/Analyze with an own-words / inference / prediction component**, never collapsing to copy-the-line recall. **Single-session lessons** (U1L3/L5/L7/L8, U3L2, U5L5) read the band directly at the lower-to-mid end. Bloom names in **English** on the plan surface (Remember / Understand / Apply / Analyze / Evaluate / Create).

### 1.4 Example Bank conventions (≥3 per teaching point — REF-02 §4.4)
Words / short phrases / short sentences, concrete, age-banded, drawn from the lesson's theme vocabulary where possible (so examples double as required-word practice). All F-01- and curation-clean. **Islamic-resonant words welcome** `[Islamic-add]`: for a /æ/ word → *cat, hat, **Allah's** signs*; sight-word practice → *I, am, a, **Muslim***; sentence pattern → *"This is my **Qur'an**." / "I help my **mother**."*. For living-being example words, use the **word only, not a picture** (C-05).

### 1.5 Discovery-pattern library (≥3 per lesson; pick from these — REF-02 §4.5)
1. **Flashcard-led** (prior sounds/sight-words → new; Hook). · 2. **Object-led** (a real object whose name carries the target — no animal depiction). · 3. **Sound-sorting-led** (initial/final/**middle** sound — the C3 phonics pattern, U4). · 4. **Story-led** (predict the next event / name the moral — reframe any neutral fable through an Islamic-akhlaq lens; U8 Ant & Grasshopper). · 5. **Question-led** ("why / what would happen if…" before content; comprehension, free-thinking). · 6. **Think-pair-share / paired exploration** (think alone → pair → present; the NCF collaborative manner — the **free-thinking pattern on the oral side**; the warrant for 🟦 SPEAKING). · 7. **Picture-to-sentence** (build sentences from a scene; SENTENCE → guided writing). · 8. **Own-way prompt** ("write/say in your own way"; the **free-thinking pattern on the writing side**; 🟦 FREEWRITE). · 9. **Role-reading / dialogue-reading as oneself** (read assigned lines aloud **as yourself**, never impersonating a character — the C-19 boundary; the standard fix for every "act out / play the roles"). · 10. **Learner-question / title-prediction** (pupils generate their own questions / predict a title — runs lightly/orally at C3 as Flex enrichment).

> **Language Games note.** NCF builds in non-musical games (chain drill, Bingo, Memory, Kim's game, guessing). Excellent Hooks/practice — use freely, **provided no melody/tune is introduced** (C-03) and any character-impersonation is converted to "as yourself" (C-19).

### 1.6 Redirection patterns (keep the child's initiative; correct only the framing)
- *Pupil offers a song/jingle/tune as a memory aid* → "Lovely! Now let's say it together **without the tune**, in a clear chant" — keep rhythm, drop melody (C-03).
- *Pupil names a Western pop figure / cartoon as an example* → "Good try! Let's use a name **we know** — a Muslim friend's name" (C-10/F-01).
- *Pupil's own-way story drifts into a festival / temple / idolatry frame* → acknowledge the effort, **redirect the setting** to a permissible one (Eid, family, a halal trip, nature as Allah's signs) while keeping the child's idea (C-01/C-04).
- *Pupil personifies water/rain/nature as the giver of life* (Uses of water, U7) → "**Who** sends the rain / makes water a gift?" — re-anchor causation in Allah (C-09/F-06). *(The main delivery-side alignment move in English.)*
- *Pupil wants to "be" a character / act it out* → redirect to **role-reading as oneself** or genuine real-exchange practice (C-19).

### 1.7 English Exit-Check Bank (≥2 ready forms per objective type; run per-student — the *same* check observed for each child, never one question per child)
| Objective type | Ready forms (pick one; per-student readout) |
|---|---|
| Phonics — middle sound (`PHONICS`, U4) | "Say a word with this **middle** sound" · sort 3 picture/word cards by middle sound |
| Sight / content word (`VOCAB`) | "Read this word" · "Use this word in a short spoken phrase" |
| Sentence (`SENTENCE`) | "Read this sentence aloud" · "Look at the picture and write/say one small sentence" |
| Read-aloud (`READALOUD`) | "Read this line aloud **with stress / intonation**" (4.2.1) · "…with the right pause at the full-stop/comma" (4.2.2) |
| Numbers — read/write (`NUMERACY`) | "Read this number aloud" (figures 4.1.4 / words 4.1.5) · "Write this number in figures / in words" (7.2.1/7.2.2) · ordinals (7.2.3, U4L6) |
| Listening 🟦 (`LISTENING`) | "Tell me **in your own words** what I just said" · pair: each tells partner, then teacher |
| Conversation 🟦 (`SPEAKING`) | "Ask your partner one question, then tell me their answer" · "Describe this in two sentences" (assess **participation + on-topic**, not perfection) |
| Greeting / farewell (`SPEAKING`/`SOCIAL`) | "Greet me politely" (3.2.1) · "Say goodbye politely to your partner" (3.2.2) |
| Command / instruction / request (`COMMAND`) | "Do what I say" (1.3.1) · "Give your partner one polite instruction, then carry out theirs" |
| Rhyme / poem (`RHYME`/`POEM`) | "Say the first two lines" (spoken, SR4) |
| Story / comprehension (`COMPREHENSION`) | one targeted "what / why" per child · or "Tell me in one sentence, **your own way**, what happened" (own-way doubles as a free-think check) |
| Guided writing (`GUIDEDWRITE`, U2/U5/U6) | "Write one guided sentence about your family/day/habit" (7.4.1/7.4.2) |
| Free / own-way writing 🟦 (`FREEWRITE`) | "Write / say one line **in your own way**" — assess **effort + on-topic**, not correctness (Create-level) |

*(Materials on every check: whiteboard + marker for the teacher; exercise book / pencil / pen (খাতা/পেনসিল/কলম) for pupils — slate / chalk are not used at the school, D-049.)*

## §4 — C3 emphasis (REF-03 §5.3)

**Framework:** C3 is **NCF 2021**, the same framework as its neighbours — **no zigzag, no special handoff smoothing** (the C3 English edition was read correctly the first time — the explicit contrast with C3 Bangla). **Master (High):** **reading comprehension is the DOMINANT C3 strand** (`5.1.x`, the spine of the whole class) — emphasise genuine understanding (own-words, prediction), not copy-the-line; **middle-sound phonics** (U4, *Era-B-new*); **guided sentence construction** (U2/U5/U6). **Protect (🟦):** **speaking** (conversation, `3.4`) and **free/own-way writing** within guided tasks — never the first to cut when short on time. **Free-think facet (emphasis, not a tag):** learner-questions / Language Games (no melody — C-03). **Deeper-not-faster:** an advanced class enriches *within* C3 (richer comprehension prompts, more peer sharing, deeper inference, more own-way writing) — **never** formalise free-writing into timed essays early, and never accelerate into the C4 named-grammar Spine. **There is NO Islamic-Universal-Core PROTECTED anchor in English** — Islamic alignment is carried through **Project 05 replacement** (the U8L2 festival passage; the name layer; the singing-verb rewords) and through **delivery** (adab/akhlaq in commands/requests U3; signs-of-Allah framing in the water texts U7; akhlaq-lens fable morals U8L1). **Curation watch:** Facts & Fables (U8) — reframe the moral through an Islamic-akhlaq lens; the hill-festival lesson and water texts → signs-of-Allah framing (C-09).
