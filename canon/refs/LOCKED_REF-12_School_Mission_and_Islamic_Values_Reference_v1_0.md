## §0 At-a-glance summary

**What this is.** The single canonical statement of *why* School for Community Development (SCD), Sylhet exists, what it is trying to build in a child, and the Islamic values every piece of curriculum, every replacement, and every classroom must reflect. Before this document existed, this guidance was implicit in the Project 00 Instructions §7; REF-12 makes it a standalone, citable reference so downstream Projects (especially Project 05) can consume it directly.

**Who uses it, and for what.**
- **Curation (Project 02 / REF-01):** REF-12 is the value-set that REF-01's categories operationalize. When you ask "*why* does this need replacing?", the answer lives here.
- **Replacement content (Project 05):** every replacement must positively reflect the values in §5–§7, not merely avoid the prohibited.
- **Teacher training (Project 07):** §8 is the values spine of teacher conduct.
- **All Projects:** the mission (§3) and philosophy (§4) are the background against which any decision is sanity-checked.

**The mission, in one sentence.** To raise children as committed Muslims by giving worldly education and afterlife-focused consciousness a proper, integrated foundation — built before the age of twelve, rooted in tawhid and the authentic Sunnah, and aimed at producing a *Muhsin*: one who does everything beautifully for the sake of Allah.

**Core values at a glance** (full detail in §5):
1. **Tawhid first** — obedience to Allah is the highest priority; everything else is ordered under it.
2. **Salafi methodology (D-020)** — Quran, authentic Sunnah, the understanding of the Salaf al-Salih.
3. **Worldly + akhirah synthesis** — secular subjects taught, never as a rival framework to the Deen.
4. **Free thinking within tawhid** — inquiry, self-discovery, and independent problem-solving, not rote recall as default.
5. **Ihsan / becoming a Muhsin** — character and excellence-for-Allah outrank marks.
6. **Adab and akhlaq** — respect (especially for teachers), honesty, gratitude, contentment, modesty (purdah).
7. **Quran-centred identity** — Tajweed, hifz, and Arabic toward understanding the Quran.
8. **Home–school partnership** — parents are the primary environment; the school is a minority of the child's time.
9. **Rootedness, not imitation** — gratitude for one's own land and ummah; no glamorising of Western life or "win-at-any-cost" competition.

> **Where REF-12 ends and REF-01 begins.** REF-12 names the values. REF-01 turns them into the C-category replacement rules, and REF-21 is the how-to-find layer for those rules. If a request is "*should* this be changed and how exactly?" → REF-01/REF-21. If it is "*what does the school stand for and why?*" → REF-12.

## §1 Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
REF-12 exists so that the school's mission and values are stated once, canonically, and consumed by every Project that needs them — rather than re-derived chat-by-chat or left implicit in Instructions §7. It is the upstream charter for the curation and replacement work.

### 1.2 What REF-12 is *not*
- It is **not** the Curation Policy. It does not list categories, tags, or replacement mechanics — that is REF-01 (operationalization) and REF-21 (trigger detection).
- It is **not** an operations manual. Fees, dress code, exam weighting, admission, and discipline procedure live in the **School Handbook**, not here. REF-12 distils only the *values* such documents express, not their administrative rules.
- It is **not** a curriculum map. Subject sequencing lives in REF-19 and the Subject Spine Playbooks.

### 1.3 Scope note — institution vs. active initiative
The founding documents describe the *full institution*, which has expanded one class per year and now extends toward SSC (Class 10) across a wide subject list. The **current curriculum initiative's active scope is Nursery, KG, and Classes 1–5** (Project 00 Instructions §1). REF-12 records the mission as the school itself states it (whole-institution), while curriculum production stays paced to the active class range. The two are not in conflict: the mission is the destination; the initiative is the part of the road currently under construction.

## §2 The school: identity and provenance

**School for Community Development (SCD)**, Sylhet branch — a fully non-profit institution established, in the school's own words, as a "community service" to raise Muslim children well. It operates under the **Institute for Community Development (ICD)**; an ICD sub-committee serves as the SCD Managing Committee with overall responsibility for administration and management. Academics are run by professionally skilled, experienced teachers; a separate team handles administration, accounts, and security. Curriculum development is an ongoing, research-based, long-term programme led by an ICD team, refined continually against (a) the ideological requirements of Islam and (b) the realistic demands of permissible (non-haram) worldly matters.

The mission and values in this reference are distilled from the school's authentic founding documents (§10), signed by the Convener of SCD / Chairman of ICD.

## §3 Mission and vision

**The problem the school was founded to answer.** In recent decades many institutions established in the name of Islamic education drifted, in the end, into imitating culture and values that conflict with Islam. SCD's founding commitment is to avoid that conformity and present a genuinely excellent education in its place.

**Vision.** To uphold, in all circumstances, the supremacy of the eternal Islamic order Allah has revealed — and to build in each child a proper synthesis of worldly education with an afterlife-focused life-consciousness and Islamic values.

**Mission.** To give the highest priority to obedience to Allah, and on that basis to reject prevailing or visible errors of *aqidah* and any other wrong matters; to give children a foundational Islamic education (the Quran's Arabic, Tajweed, the basics of the Deen) alongside Bangla, English, Mathematics, and the sciences; and to bring each child, *before the age of twelve*, to stand on a firm foundation of Islamic knowledge and values.

**The kind of family the school addresses.** Thoughtful, inquisitive guardians who are committed to living life according to Allah's law — and who are willing to partner with the school rather than outsource their child's upbringing to it.

## §4 Educational philosophy

### 4.1 Synthesis, not separation
Worldly ("secular") subjects are taught seriously and well — but never as a self-standing framework that rivals the Deen. The aim is integration: knowledge of the world held inside a God-conscious, akhirah-aware frame. For history, the school begins with Islam's own heritage; for environmental science, it teaches the natural world as a field of the *signs (āyāt)* Allah points to in the Quran. Secular content is the body; tawhid is the spine.

### 4.2 The 1–12 critical-formation window
The years before twelve are treated as the decisive, sensitive period in which character and *aqidah* are formed. If wrong belief or values are planted then, they are very hard to remove later. The whole design — early Quranic grounding, careful curation of inputs, a protected environment — flows from this single conviction: **build the foundation before the window closes.** (This is also why the school's curation workload is front-loaded onto the primary years, which Project 00 serves.)

### 4.3 Free thinking and self-discovery — within tawhid
The school does not want passive, rote learners. It wants children who think, inquire, try things for themselves, and solve problems — and who, precisely because their foundation is secure, can do so safely. This is the operating instruction behind Project 00 Instructions §6: **student-facing material should encourage free thinking, self-discovery, and independent problem-solving within an Islamic framework — not rote recall as the default.** Inquiry is welcomed; it is anchored, not unmoored.

### 4.4 National curriculum, Islamically curated; halal livelihood; rootedness
Students follow the national curriculum so they can succeed in halal employment and clean living — but that curriculum is curated against Islamic values (the work REF-01 governs). The school explicitly refuses to sell children the dream of emigrating to the West or the selfish dream of "being well alone." Instead it teaches gratitude for the land Allah decreed for them and a life lived well *with* their own community and ummah. Outcomes preserved; framing made Islamic.

## §5 Core Islamic values

These are the values every curated lesson, every replacement, and every classroom is expected to embody. They are stated here; REF-01 turns the *avoidance* side of several of them into concrete replacement rules.

### 5.1 Tawhid and obedience to Allah as the highest priority
Everything is ordered under the obedience of Allah. Worldly success, marks, and recognition are subordinate goods, never the summit.

### 5.2 Salafi methodology (D-020)
The school's interpretive lens is the Quran as primary source, the authentic (sahih/hasan) Sunnah, and the understanding of the Salaf al-Salih (the first three generations); positions are weighed on evidence, with no default of madhhab taqlid. **Operational consequence (carried into REF-01 §1.3):** because mainstream local content reflects a broader Sunni-Hanafi-Sufi cultural Islam, the replacement workload is *higher* than a general cultural framing would require — practices treated as bid'ah (mawlid/milad, urs and saint veneration, tawassul through the deceased, grave devotion, music in an Islamic frame, devotional poetry carrying tariqah lineages) are curated out. Plan capacity accordingly.

### 5.3 Charitable in attitude, distinct in practice
The school is charitable toward Muslims who hold differing practices; it simply does not platform those practices in its own curriculum. Values content corrects framing **without disparaging people or other communities.** (This posture is binding on Project 05 replacement work — mirror of REF-01 §1.4.)

### 5.4 Ihsan — and the goal of the *Muhsin*
The school's stated summit for a human being is *Ihsan*: in its own founding phrase, **doing things beautifully for the sake of Allah.** A child raised here is meant to grow toward becoming a *Muhsin* — someone established on Ihsan — for whom excellence and beauty in every act is worship, not performance for reward.

### 5.5 Honesty and character over marks
Truthfulness, honesty, and beautiful character are explicitly valued *above* high marks. Dishonest shortcuts to success are treated with zero tolerance; just, Islamically-sound conduct is presented as the only real success — even when it is the harder road.

### 5.6 Adab toward teachers
At this age children adopt teachers as model, imitable figures; the dignity of the teacher in Islam (illustrated through the lives of the Sahaba, the Tābi'īn, and the four Imams) is therefore a protected value. This shapes both teacher conduct (§8) and the home partnership (§7): backbiting or belittling a teacher in front of a child is treated as actively harmful.

### 5.7 Quran-centred identity
A Muslim identity built on the Book is core, not optional. Concretely (see §6): Tajweed and *nazera* first, then a chosen hifz track, and an Arabic programme aimed at *understanding* the Quran.

### 5.8 Modesty and gender propriety (purdah)
Modesty and observance of purdah are upheld with care across the school. Practical expressions include same-gender grouping beyond the mahram circle and caution around mixed settings — values that REF-01 operationalizes (e.g. its mixed-gender depiction rule).

### 5.9 Gratitude, contentment, anti-materialism
Children are to be raised grateful and content — taught to hear "no" to excessive demands, so they do not grow ungrateful, selfish, or intolerant. Plainness over fancy display is a stated value (e.g. the handbook's preference for clean-not-expensive materials).

### 5.10 Brotherhood over rivalry
The "win at any cost" ethic is explicitly rejected. Children are not to be set against their Muslim brothers and sisters in hatred, envy, or contempt to climb ahead; they are raised to do every work well for Allah's pleasure, within an ummah-first frame rather than a nation-over-ummah one.

## §6 The Quran and Arabic at the centre

Held here as an *identity* marker (the full sequence lives in the curriculum maps, not REF-12):

- **Tajweed & nazera first**, then hifz. Foundational Qaida/Amma-para work in the earliest years; Quran reading (nazera) by the early primary classes; a chosen hifz track thereafter.
- **A chosen hifz track** — tilāwah (regular recitation), 10-para, or 30-para — selected per the child's capacity, pursued across the school years.
- **Arabic toward understanding** — the stated aim of the Arabic programme is that a student can, in time, render and grasp the Quran's meaning, not merely decode letters.

The point for downstream Projects: Quran and Arabic are not "a subject among subjects" in the school's self-understanding; they are the centre of gravity the other subjects orbit.

## §7 Home–school partnership values

The school holds a clear, quantified humility about its own reach: a child spends only a *minority* of the year with the school (the founding documents put it near ~11%) and the great majority in the home and surrounding environment. The stated consequence: **if the home environment is not sound, no institution can secure a child's character.** The partnership values that follow from this — distilled from *Suggestions for Parents* — are part of the school's value-set, and the substrate for any parent-facing work (Project 06/07):

- **Parents as primary formers** — du'a for the child (including aloud, in front of them), teaching the child to ask of Allah, and modelling the Deen.
- **Protecting the input stream** — guarding children from harmful media (smartphone/internet/TV exposure, and the documented dangers thereof) during the formation years, because curation at school cannot survive an uncurated home.
- **Honouring the teacher** — never backbiting or belittling teachers before the child (§5.6).
- **Healthy body, healthy attention** — early sleep, a proper breakfast, real play, and regular contact with nature as part of wellbeing.
- **A reading habit** — chosen books: prophets' and companions' biographies, travel, wholesome adventure.
- **Friendship and openness** — being close enough that a child brings any harmful encounter to the parent first.
- **Safeguarding** — caution around non-mahram contact, lap-sitting by outsiders, isolation with helpers/drivers, and bed-sharing past the age the Sunnah specifies separate bedding.
- **Gratitude over indulgence** — teaching "no," so the child grows content rather than entitled (§5.9).

REF-12 records these as values; their *delivery* (parent comms, trackers, follow-up) is Project 06, and their *teaching* is Project 07.

## §8 Teacher and staff conduct values

The full operational code is the *Teacher–Staff Code of Conduct* (v1.2). REF-12 holds only its **values spine**, as the charter for Project 07:

- **Relate everything to the Quran and Sunnah** — the school's central aim in the classroom is to present Islam's main current to the children; teachers connect each subject back to it.
- **An Islamic register** — Islamic phrases (*Bismillah, alhamdulillah, mā shā Allah, in shā Allah, subhānallah*) become part of natural speech; Western/foreign idioms and expressions are avoided.
- **Respect in address and manner** — respectful "āpni"-form address, never the demeaning "tui"; salam on entry and exit; correction always for the child's benefit, never in anger or to humiliate.
- **Character and effort over marks** — honesty, truthfulness, cleanliness, and extra effort are highlighted and (within school rules) rewarded; un-Islamic shortcuts are never presented as success (§5.5).
- **Dignity of every child** — no public shaming, no comparison or contempt over a child's face, family, or finances; safe, secure, bully-free classrooms (zero tolerance for bullying).
- **Professionalism and confidentiality** — sensitive school matters are not aired outside; parent discussions follow the official process and the school's policy over personal ties.

The teaching method itself is expected to serve §4.3: use the board, sketches, and visual explanation; vary methods; draw the child toward understanding rather than dictation.

## §11 Values-alignment checklist

Run any curriculum element, replacement, or training artifact against this before approval. (This complements — does not replace — the REF-01 Executive Checklist, which governs the *replacement mechanics*. This checklist governs the *values fit*.)

**Foundation**
- [ ] Does it keep **obedience to Allah / tawhid** as the ordering frame, with worldly content held inside it — not as a rival framework? (§4.1, §5.1)
- [ ] Is it consistent with the **Salafi methodology (D-020)** — and free of bid'ah-type practice presented as norm? (§5.2)

**Child and character**
- [ ] Does student-facing content invite **thinking, inquiry, and self-discovery** rather than default to rote recall? (§4.3)
- [ ] Does it cultivate **Ihsan / character / honesty** above marks and reward? (§5.4, §5.5)
- [ ] Does it model **gratitude, contentment, and brotherhood** — never entitlement or win-at-any-cost rivalry? (§5.9, §5.10)

**Adab, modesty, identity**
- [ ] Does it uphold **adab** (especially toward teachers) and an **Islamic register**, free of foreign idiom presented as aspirational? (§5.6, §8)
- [ ] Does it observe **modesty / purdah and gender propriety** in any depiction or grouping? (§5.8)
- [ ] Does it honour the **Quran-centred identity** where relevant? (§5.7, §6)

**Posture and partnership**
- [ ] Is it **charitable in attitude** — correcting framing without disparaging people or communities? (§5.3)
- [ ] Where parent-facing, does it respect the **home as primary** and the school's partnership values? (§7)

**Positive replacement test (Project 05)**
- [ ] If this is replacement content, does it **positively reflect** an Islamic value (family life, prophetic narration, adab/akhlaq, signs of Allah in nature, halal work) — not merely avoid the prohibited? (§9)

> If any box cannot be ticked, the element is not values-aligned. Route mechanics questions to REF-01/REF-21; route genuine values questions or unclear cases to the Principal.
