## §1 — Why It Matters (60 seconds)

The same chapter can produce a *syllabus-covered* question ("What is photosynthesis?") or a *thinking-developing* one ("A plant is kept in a dark room for five days — predict what happens and explain why"). The topic is identical; the **cognitive demand** differs. Bloom's names that demand so teaching and testing aim at it on purpose.

Memorisation is the foundation, not the enemy — a student cannot reason about zakat they have never learned. The aim is to *use* memorised knowledge for thinking, which is exactly what scholarship exams, Britti, Olympiad-style papers, and real life test. For an Islamic school this is native ground: the Qur'an repeatedly calls to reflection (تفكر), reasoning (تعقل), and pondering (تدبر) — education that produces students who remember correctly, understand deeply, reason carefully, and act with adab.

## §2 — The Six Levels and How to Tell Them Apart

| Level | Bangla | Mental work | The question asks… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Remember** | মনে রাখা | Recall / recognise facts, terms, rules, formulas, texts | "Can the student bring it back from memory?" |
| **Understand** | বোঝা | Explain, summarise, classify, give examples, restate in own words | "Does the student know what this means?" |
| **Apply** | প্রয়োগ | Use a learned rule / formula / method in a situation | "Can the student use what they learned?" |
| **Analyze** | বিশ্লেষণ | Break into parts; compare; find cause, pattern, relationship | "Can the student see the structure / cause / pattern?" |
| **Evaluate** | মূল্যায়ন | Judge using criteria, evidence, reasons | "Can the student make a reasoned judgment?" |
| **Create** | সৃষ্টি | Produce something new by combining knowledge + judgment | "Can the student make something meaningful and original?" |

The hard part is the **boundary** between adjacent levels. These four contrasts are where most mis-levelling happens:

**Remember vs Understand**
| Remember | Understand |
| --- | --- |
| Recalls exact information. | Explains the meaning. |
| "What is evaporation?" | "Why does water disappear from wet clothes?" |
| "Write the definition of noun." | "Explain why 'Karim' is a noun." |

**Understand vs Apply**
| Understand | Apply |
| --- | --- |
| Explains the rule. | Uses the rule. |
| "Explain what a verb is." | "Underline the verbs in these sentences." |
| "Explain the area formula." | "Find the area of this rectangle." |

**Apply vs Analyze**
| Apply | Analyze |
| --- | --- |
| Uses a known rule / method (clear procedure). | Breaks information down and studies relationships (examines first). |
| "Find the area of the rectangle." | "Compare two fields' areas and explain why one needs more fencing." |
| "Correct the sentence." | "Identify the errors and group them by grammar rule." |

**Analyze vs Evaluate vs Create**
| Analyze | Evaluate | Create |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Examines parts and relationships. | Judges, using criteria. | Produces a new response / plan / product. |
| "Compare two methods." | "Which method is better here? Explain." | "Propose your own method." |
| "Find the causes." | "Which cause is most serious? Justify." | "Design a plan to address it." |

Remember is the foundation — there is no level below it (but recall ≠ copying: recall is *without looking*).

## §5 — Class Targets

### 5.1 Dominant levels by class (school runs to Class 5)
| Class | Dominant levels | Suitable higher-level exposure | Practical note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Remember, Understand | simple Apply, simple Create | pictures, objects, oral answers, matching, short sentences |
| 2 | Remember, Understand, simple Apply | simple Analyze (grouping) | classify, match, use words, simple story sums |
| 3 | Understand, Apply | simple Analyze, guided Create | begin "why / how / compare" on familiar content |
| 4 | Understand, Apply | Analyze, simple Evaluate | tables, diagrams, short explanations, real-life problems |
| 5 | Apply, Understand, Analyze | Evaluate, guided Create | scholarship-style structured reasoning |

*(Classes 6–10 bands are in REF-06 §3.2 for reference as the school expands.)*

### 5.2 Indicative % mix — **chapter scope**
| Class group | Remember | Understand | Apply | Analyze | Evaluate | Create |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Class 1–2 | 35–45% | 30–40% | 15–25% | 0–10% | 0–5% | 0–5% |
| Class 3–5 | 20–30% | 25–35% | 25–35% | 10–20% | 0–10% | 0–10% |

> **Chapter, not session (D-050).** These bands describe the whole chapter, however many periods it spans. Across a multi-period chapter the mix *climbs*: early sessions sit at the lower end, later sessions reach into Apply / Analyze as the band allows. A single-period chapter collapses to one session and the band is read directly. Per-session focus lives in the Session Map (REF-02 §2.9). Bands are indicative; exact mark splits come later from the Tier 1 / Tier 2 question-setting guidelines.

**Age rule both ways.** Do not *under-demand* older students — definition-only diets weaken them. Do not *over-demand* younger ones — keep language and context simple. A Class 1 child *can* evaluate: "Was it good that Ayesha helped her friend? Why?" The level is high; the wording is simple.

## §6 — Lesson Planning: The Ladder

A lesson should not sit at one level. Build it as a ladder that climbs from foundation.

**How to build a lesson ladder (stepwise):**
1. State the learning goal in one sentence.
2. **Remember** — open by recalling the facts/terms the lesson needs.
3. **Understand** — build meaning: explain the concept, give examples, have students restate it in their own words.
4. **Apply** — students use the concept (solve, correct, demonstrate).
5. **Analyze** — students compare, classify, or find the cause/pattern.
6. **Evaluate** — students judge "which is better / more correct" with reasons.
7. **Create** — students produce a small original response.
8. Not every lesson must reach Create; but across a week students should meet more than one level.

**Sample weekly structure (Sunday–Thursday fits the normal week):**
| Day | Focus | Bloom's level | Activity | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduce concept | Remember, Understand | explanation, vocabulary, examples | oral recall + short explanation |
| 2 | Guided practice | Understand, Apply | pair work, board practice | classwork check |
| 3 | Independent practice | Apply | written exercises | short written task |
| 4 | Deeper thinking | Analyze, Evaluate | compare, classify, justify | group discussion / short answer |
| 5 | Consolidation | Mixed | quiz, correction, mini-project | homework or short test |

**Oral questioning matters too** — vary it by level rather than relying on chorus answers (chorus suits memorisation; individual and group reasoning questions build deeper learning): Remember "What does this word mean?" → Understand "Explain it in your own words." → Apply "Use it in a sentence." → Analyze "Which part shows the action?" → Evaluate "Which sentence is better? Why?" → Create "Make your own sentence."

**Worked model — Class 3 Math, Addition with Carrying** (goal: add 2–3-digit numbers with carrying *and explain why carrying is needed*):
| Day | Focus | Level | Student does | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | Review place value | Remember/Understand | identify ones/tens/hundreds | labels place values correctly |
| Mon | Introduce carrying | Understand | explain why 12 ones → 1 ten + 2 ones (sticks / chart) | explains carrying orally |
| Tue | Guided practice | Apply | solve similar sums | correct method shown |
| Wed | Word problems | Apply/Analyze | choose relevant numbers, then solve | identifies relevant information |
| Thu | Error correction + mini-create | Analyze/Create | find the error in a wrong solution; write own story sum | explains error + writes own problem |

Matching sample questions, by level: Remember "Ten ones make how many tens? / ১০টি একক মিলে কত দশক?" · Understand "Why carry 1 when adding 8 + 7? / ৮+৭ করলে হাতে ১ রাখি কেন?" · Apply "Add 48 + 37 / ৪৮+৩৭ যোগ করো" · Analyze "Find the mistake: 58 + 26 = 74 / ভুল বের করো" · Evaluate "Which method is easier — vertical, or tens-and-ones separately? Why?" · Create "Create your own addition story problem / নিজের একটি যোগের গল্প-সমস্যা তৈরি করো."

## §7 — Question Setting

**Align teaching and testing.** If you teach at Remember but test at Analyze, students feel unfairly treated; if you teach deeply but test only memory, students stop valuing depth. Each lesson activity should have a matching exam-question type (practise definitions → some Remember items; explain in own words → Understand; solve → Apply; compare/classify → Analyze; justify → Evaluate; design/write original → Create).

### 7.1 Build a question-paper blueprint (stepwise)
A blueprint is a planning grid filled in *before* writing the paper. It balances five things: topic, marks, Bloom's level, difficulty, and question type.
1. List the units/topics to be tested, down the left.
2. Decide total marks per unit (by teaching weight).
3. For each unit, split its marks across the six Bloom's levels, guided by the class band (§5.2).
4. Check the column totals match your target level mix for the class.
5. Then — and only then — write questions to fill each cell.

**Sample blueprint — 100 marks, 3 hours:**
| Unit | Rem | Und | App | Ana | Eva | Cre | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 0 | 24 |
| 2 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 6 | 3 | 0 | 25 |
| 3 | 3 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 28 |
| 4 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 23 |
| **Total** | **12** | **20** | **27** | **23** | **13** | **5** | **100** |

A template only — final splits depend on class, subject, and school guidelines. A paper of only low-level items inflates marks but weakens thinking; too many high-level items frustrate students if the teaching did not prepare them. Aim for **balanced rigor.**

### 7.2 Write an MCQ that tests more than recall (stepwise)
1. Decide the target level first (Remember? Apply? Analyze?).
2. For higher levels, open with a **situation**, not a bare fact.
3. Make the stem ask for the *best* answer to that situation.
4. Write **plausible distractors** — each based on a common student mistake, related to the topic, similar in length/style to the key, and requiring thought to reject.

Low-level (Remember): "What is the formula for area of a rectangle?" → A. l+w · **B. l×w** · C. l−w · D. l÷w.
Higher-level (Apply): "A mat is 6 m long and 4 m wide. Which calculation gives the floor area it covers?" → A. 6+4 · **B. 6×4** · C. 6−4 · D. 6÷4.
Weak distractors are obviously silly ("a plant in a dark room — which process is affected? Photosynthesis / Melting / Rusting / Boiling"). Strong distractors carry the misconception ("Photosynthesis decreases because light is needed / increases because darkness helps leaves / water absorption stops because roots need light / oxygen production increases because the plant rests").

### 7.3 Write a genuinely high-Bloom's creative question
A long question is not automatically high-level. *Disguised memorization:* "Read the story, then write the definition of honesty" — the answer is still a memorised line. *Genuine:* "Karim found money in the classroom and returned it. Analyze his action in light of honesty, then write two ways students can practise honesty in school." Structure a strong creative question in parts that climb:
| Part | Level | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | — | short story, table, map, diagram, or situation |
| Basic question | Remember/Understand | identify the main issue |
| Application | Apply | use a rule or concept |
| Analysis | Analyze | find cause, relationship, or evidence |
| Judgment / creation | Evaluate/Create | reasoned opinion or proposed solution |

## §8 — Bloom's Across Subjects

Bloom's applies everywhere but *looks* different per subject. One low→high contrast per subject, with the guiding note:

- **Bangla (বাংলা):** কবির নাম লেখো → কবিতার মূল ভাব নিজের ভাষায় ব্যাখ্যা করো. *Move past memorised summaries — explain, infer, compare characters, justify the moral, write original paragraphs.*
- **English:** "Write the meaning of 'honest'" → "Use 'honest' in a sentence about school life." *English-only questions are acceptable here since the subject requires English practice; explain in Bangla while teaching when needed.*
- **Mathematics (গণিত):** আয়তক্ষেত্রের ক্ষেত্রফলের সূত্র লেখো → একই ক্ষেত্রফল কিন্তু ভিন্ন দৈর্ঘ্য-প্রস্থের দুটি আয়তক্ষেত্র তৈরি করো. *Use real Bangladeshi contexts — classroom area, taka, distance, zakat calculation, market quantities.*
- **Science (বিজ্ঞান):** বাষ্পীভবন কী? → রোদে কাপড় দ্রুত শুকায় কেন? বাষ্পীভবন দিয়ে ব্যাখ্যা করো. *Science naturally supports higher levels — observe, explain, predict, compare, design; use diagrams, local environment, Sylhet weather/rivers, health.*
- **BGS (বাংলাদেশ ও বিশ্বপরিচয়):** রাজধানীর নাম লেখো → ঢাকা কেন প্রশাসনিক ও অর্থনৈতিকভাবে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ? দুটি কারণ ব্যাখ্যা করো. *Not just dates and names — build responsible citizenship and evidence-based reasoning.*
- **Islamic Studies (ইসলাম শিক্ষা):** যাকাত কী? → যাকাত কীভাবে সমাজে দরিদ্রকে সাহায্য করে? ব্যাখ্যা করো. *Accurate knowledge → understanding → practical application → moral reasoning + adab, thinking within Qur'an, Sunnah, and correct guidance.*
- **Arabic (আরবি):** "kitab" শব্দের অর্থ লেখো → "kitab" শব্দটি অর্থপূর্ণ আরবি বাক্যে ব্যবহার করো. *Move beyond word meaning toward correct usage, sentence formation, simple comprehension.*
- **ICT (তথ্য ও যোগাযোগ প্রযুক্তি):** দুটি ইনপুট ডিভাইসের নাম লেখো → নিরাপদ পাসওয়ার্ড তৈরির পাঁচটি নিয়ম তৈরি করো. *Practical by nature; keep age-appropriate and safe, school-approved contexts, no unsafe browsing tasks.*

## §9 — Free Thinking and Islamic Framing

Free thinking (স্বাধীন চিন্তা) is not careless thinking — it is observing, asking, comparing, reasoning, justifying, and expressing **with responsibility**. Analyze / Evaluate / Create are the levels that open it; a student who can evaluate resists weak information, a student who can create communicates clearly.

**Open-ended questions** allow more than one valid answer and must still be marked fairly. Use a criteria rubric (10-mark shape):
| Criteria | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Content accuracy | fully accurate | mostly | some correct | mostly incorrect |
| Reasoning | clear reasons + evidence | limited | weak | none |
| Organization | well organised | mostly | some structure | disorganised |
| Islamic / moral alignment | fully appropriate | mostly | needs correction | inappropriate |

Combined: Accuracy 3 + Reasoning 3 + Organization 2 + Value alignment 2 = **10**.

**Frame thinking inside revealed guidance** — adab with the intellect, not against it. Open the door, then anchor it:
| Unsafe framing | Better Islamic framing |
| --- | --- |
| "Say anything you want about salah." | "Explain how salah can build discipline in a student's life, using examples." |
| "Make your own rule about halal and haram." | "Explain what a Muslim should check before eating packaged food, using what you learned." |
| "Give any opinion about honesty." | "Evaluate the student's action in light of Islamic honesty and school rules." |

Free thinking and Islamic adab are not enemies — properly framed, each strengthens the other.

## §10 — Peer Review and Subject-Lead Submission

**Peer review** should be respectful and specific — not "this question is bad" but "this seems to test only recall; could we add a situation so students apply the concept?" Run each question through:
| Check | Yes / No |
| --- | --- |
| Connected to the syllabus? | |
| Bloom's level clear? | |
| Language age-appropriate? | |
| Mark allocation fair? | |
| Free of ambiguity? | |
| Aligned with Islamic values? | |
| Tests thinking, not only memorization? | |

**When submitting to the Subject Lead (বিষয় সমন্বয়কারী),** include: class · subject · chapter · question type · intended Bloom's level · expected answer (key points or rubric) · your specific concern (e.g., "Is this too difficult for Class 5?"). This makes feedback faster and sharper.

## §11 — Watch-Outs (the ten misconceptions, condensed)

1. **"Higher is always better."** False — higher levels need lower-level foundations. Use the right level for the purpose.
2. **"MCQs can only test Remember."** False — with a situation and good options they test Apply, Analyze, sometimes Evaluate.
3. **"Creative questions are automatically high-Bloom's."** False — a creative wrapper around a copied definition is still memorisation.
4. **"Levels are strictly sequential."** Mostly true but not a prison — a child may judge before formal analysis, or create a rough draft then improve it. Use Bloom's as a guide.
5. **"Only older students can think at higher levels."** False — young students analyze, evaluate, and create at simple levels ("Was it kind that Ayesha shared her book? Why?").
6. **"A high-level verb makes a high-level question."** False — the *mental work* decides the level. "Analyze photosynthesis" is vague; "Separate its inputs and outputs and explain how they connect" is real Analyze.
7. **"Bloom's is only for exams."** False — it serves lesson planning, oral questioning, homework, class tests, peer discussion, and exam review.
8. **"Higher-level questions must be very hard."** False — higher thinking can be simple and age-appropriate.
9. **"Each question has exactly one correct level."** Often a question sits between two, depending on how it is taught and marked ("Compare roots and leaves" is Understand if students list memorised differences, Analyze if they examine functions and relationships).
10. **"Bloom's reduces Islamic learning to skills only."** False — it is a thinking framework, not a replacement for purpose. Islamic education still aims at iman, adab, akhlaq, correct practice, and beneficial knowledge; Bloom's only helps teachers ask better questions so that knowledge is understood and practised more deeply.

## §13 — Ready-Check (before you finalise a plan or paper)

- ☐ Each question / teaching point has an **intended level**, and the **mental work** matches it (ran §11).
- ☐ The set fits the **class band** (§5.1) and sits within the **chapter % mix** (§5.2).
- ☐ A multi-period chapter's levels **climb** across sessions.
- ☐ For a paper: the **blueprint** is filled before writing, and column totals match the target mix.
- ☐ MCQs use **situations + plausible distractors**; creative questions are **genuinely** high-Bloom's, not disguised recall.
- ☐ Open-ended items have a **criteria rubric** and **Islamic framing** (§9).
- ☐ Memorisation is used as a **foundation**, not the whole paper.
