## §0 — Read-First Summary & Checklist (staff-facing)

**What this playbook is.** REF-02 says *what every lesson plan must contain* (the 8-field Spine, the Lesson Flow, the Flex Zone). REF-03-English says *what those fields mean for English specifically* — which content is mandatory, which words must appear, which Bloom mix fits each class, which examples and discovery patterns are pre-approved, when each topic is revised, and where each year's emphasis goes.

**What it is NOT.** It is not a lesson plan, not a curriculum, and not a re-print of REF-19. It is the subject rulebook an English lesson-plan author reads *before* writing a single lesson.

**The one English principle.** *Communication is the goal; the alphabet → vocabulary → sentence → comprehension spine carries it; free thinking is protected at every rung; and — unlike Bangla — Islamic alignment in English is built mainly through replacement (Project 05) and delivery, not through protected source content.* English's NCF framework is **flatter and more communicative** than Bangla's tall decoding ladder, so several parallel strands share the High-load spine rather than one dominant ladder.

**The two structural facts every English author must hold (the sharp contrasts with Bangla):**
1. **No Islamic-Universal-Core PROTECTED anchor exists in English.** Where Bangla protects-in-place three Islamic texts (the Khandaq narrative, the আবু বকর / উমর রা biographies, the Farewell-Hajj sermon), English's Universal Core is **communicative and secular** (greetings, family, time, travel, environment). The Islamic touchpoints English surfaces are **NEEDS-REPLACEMENT negatives** (the C4 U16–17 Hindu/Buddhist temple + Dashain cluster; the C4 U3 assembly anthem; pervasive song framings; living-being illustrations). **Implication: English's Islamic-alignment workload sits heavily on Project 05 replacement and on *delivery* (adab/akhlaq, signs-of-Allah framing), not on protect-in-place.**
2. **Grammar is *named* only from C4 (NAMED-FROM-C4).** The NCF pupil book **de-labels** grammar that the Teacher Guide names. The **TG, not the de-labelled pupil book, is the source of truth for objectives.**

## §2 — The English Spine (specializing REF-02 §2)

REF-02's Spine has eight numbered fields. This section gives the English form of each. Fields REF-02 already fully specifies (§2.4 mechanics, §2.7/§2.8 homework structure) carry only English notes; the fields REF-02 defers to the Subject Spine Playbook (§2.2, §2.3) carry the substantive specialization.

### 2.1 Learning Objective — English verbs + the NCF English competency framework

REF-02 §2.1 requires one observable verb matching the §2.3 Bloom level. For English:

- **Observable English verb modes** (pick the mode the objective is checkable in): oral (*say, ask, answer, greet, describe, recite, read aloud*), written (*write, copy, label, fill in, compose*), graphic/receptive (*point to, match, sort, circle, follow*), produced artifact (*write/say in your own way, present your thinking* — the free-thinking mode).
- **The NCF English subject-wise-competency framework is the objective vocabulary** *(§5.B — all five English TGs, "adopt the NCF English competency framework").* NCF defines **9 subject-wise English competencies** (C1's unit openers surface 11 class-wise blocks that map onto the same skills + a values layer). Every NCF English unit carries one or more competency codes and 3-part learning-outcome codes (`competency.theme.outcome`, e.g. `3.2.1`); the author **takes the objective from the unit's TG-validated codes** rather than inventing one. **This is the English sibling of the Bangla যোগ্যতা framework — same listen/speak/read/write/mechanics architecture, stated in English competency codes, with two English-distinctive strand families: a dedicated phonics strand (1.1.x / 3.1.x) and an explicit "vocabulary in use" strand (8.1.x).** The recurring strands across the five classes:

| Strand (skill) | Indicative codes | What it covers | Class span |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** Listening | 1.1 (sounds/phonics), 1.2 (greetings/dialogue), 1.3 (commands), 1.4 (questions), 1.5 (texts/fables), 1.6 (announcements/info) | follow sounds, greetings, commands, conversation, announcements | C1–C5 |
| **2** Listening to literary text | 2.1 | listen to rhymes/poems/digital content with enjoyment | C1–C5 |
| **3** Speaking | 3.1 (say sounds), 3.2 (greet), 3.3 (commands/requests), 3.4 (ask/answer, converse), 3.5 (describe orally) | say sounds, greet, ask/answer, **converse (3.4)**, describe fluently | C1–C5 |
| **4** Reading aloud | 4.1 (numbers/figures-words; time/currency), 4.2 (sentences/texts with stress & intonation) | read numbers; read aloud with prosody | C1–C5 |
| **5** Reading comprehension | 5.1 (meaning of words; specific information; **inferred meaning**) | the dominant English strand — literal → information → inference | C2–C5 |
| **6** Reading literary text | 6.1 | read & appreciate rhymes/poems/stories/fables for enjoyment | C1–C5 |
| **7** Writing | 7.1/7.2 (mechanics + numbers), **7.3 (correspondence / form-filling)**, 7.4 (composition, letters) | mechanics → guided → functional (7.3) → composition (7.4) | C1–C5 |

> **Rule E-2 — the named-from-C4 grammar rule** *(§5.B — C4/C5; REF-19 Flag `NAMED-FROM-C4`).* Grammar, tense, and Wh-questions are taught **implicitly C1–C3** and **named** from C4. The NCF **pupil book de-labels** grammar that the **TG names**. **The objective is read from the TG, not the de-labelled pupil book.** For C1–C3, teach correctness-in-use (no grammar labels in the objective); from C4, name the construct on the NCF schedule (articles/pronouns/nouns/past tense at C4; capitals/articles/parts-of-speech/future tense at C5).

### 2.2 Must-Cover Content — the communicative spine + vocabulary

This is the English form of REF-02 §2.2 (D-021). English Must-Cover Content has two parts: **(A) the spine rung** the lesson sits on, and **(B) the required vocabulary**.

**(A) The English load-bearing spine.** English's spine is **flatter** than Bangla's single tall decoding ladder — several High-load strands run in parallel — but the foundational order still holds (REF-19 load-bearing + the C1→C2 critical handoff):

| Rung | Element | Where formally taught | Load-bearing (REF-19) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Alphabet & letter-sounds** (`ENG-ALPHABET`) | C1 *full acquisition* (U02, all 26 letters; non-cursive air-trace-then-write); C2 review/consolidate (U02) | 🔴 High (most load-bearing topic) |
| 2 | **Phonics / sound play** (`ENG-PHONICS`) | C1 *precursor only* (U05 rhymes & letter-sounds); **C2 systematic initial/final sounds — GENUINELY NEW in Era B** (U06); C3 middle sounds (U04); C4 stress (U06); C5 teacher-side only | 🟠 Medium |
| 3 | **Vocabulary** (`ENG-VOCAB`) | C1 *30-word Universal Core* (8 sight + 7 action + 15 content); C2–C5 distributed per-unit "vocabulary in use" (8.1); C5 word-formation (U13, 8.2) | 🔴 High |
| 4 | **Sentence construction** (`ENG-SENTENCE`) | C1 fixed templates; C2 read/write + structure-awareness; C3 guided; C4–C5 composition base | 🔴 High |
| 5 | **Reading comprehension** (`ENG-COMPREHENSION`) | C2 literal; C3 *dominant strand*; C4–C5 inference + main-idea (the **backbone**, 16/20 C5 units) | 🔴 High |

> **Rule E-1 — the phonics-handoff rule** *(§5.B — C1/C2; REF-19 Flag `critical handoff`).*
> **C1:** phonics is **precursor only** — letter-sounds + rhyme-based sound awareness (U05, "Rhymes and Sounds"); **there is no systematic phonics at C1.**
> **C2:** systematic **initial/final-sound** phonics is **first introduced** (U06, "Let's Play with Sounds," new competency 1.1.x / 3.1.x) — **no Era-A antecedent.**
> **C3:** extends to **middle sounds** (U04, 1.1.2 / 3.1.2).
> **C4:** stress at syllable level (U06).
> **Do not pre-teach** systematic phonics in C1. Confirm **C1 letter-sound mastery** before the C2 step — this is the highest-value early-years checkpoint, the English analogue of the Bangla alphabet→conjunct handoff.

**Mechanics review-vs-teach handoff.** As in Bangla, some early rungs are *consolidation* not *first-teaching*: C2 U02 alphabet is **review-and-consolidate** (not re-teach); the C1 30-word core is taught as a *list* while NCF distributes vocabulary per-lesson. Set the objective accordingly — a review/consolidate lesson's objective is recall/consolidation, not first-teaching.

**(B) Required vocabulary** (REF-01 §4.2/§4.3 — word-level, position-free):
- **C1:** the **30-word Universal Core** (8 sight words + 7 action words + 15 content words), delivered as a **consolidated list**. This is a **school asset** — keep teaching from it even though NCF distributes vocabulary per-unit. Replacement content reuses the core words wherever the source text allows.
- **C2–C5:** per-unit themed "vocabulary in use" (8.1; NCF distributed). **The controlled glossary is dropped in the 2026 books** (Era-A p.103 removed; the C5 TG instructs per-session pre-teaching + dictionary look-up, U18). **Recommendation (from the C5 reconciliation): rebuild a controlled running word-bank locally across classes** — the direct parallel to the Bangla 99-word-core finding. The required-word list for any lesson is its TG theme vocabulary plus any earlier-tier core word the lesson reuses.

**Rules (English-specific, in addition to REF-02 §2.2 rules):**
1. The spine order holds: a lesson may not assume systematic phonics before C2, or named grammar before C4 (rules E-1, E-2).
2. Keep C1 sentence work concrete (picture-prompted, real objects); keep C1 handwriting **non-cursive air-trace-then-write** (the deliberate Era-3 method).
3. F-01 holds on every word and example (no Western names/foods/clothing — replace with Bengali-Muslim equivalents).

### 2.3 Bloom's-Level Distribution — English per class band

- **Class 1–2 band** (from REF-02 §2.3 / REF-06 §3.6): Remember 35–45%, Understand 30–40%, Apply 15–25%, Analyze 0–10%, Evaluate 0–5%, Create 0–5%. First-introduction alphabet/sound lessons may be Remember-dominant with justification; the **free-writing / own-way-expression lessons reach into Create even at C1–2** and are explicitly permitted there (they are the PROTECTED strand — §3.4).
- **Class 3–5 bands:** use **REF-06 §3.6** for the exact indicative shares (recall share falls and Analyze/Evaluate/Create rise as the class rises). Not re-printed here to avoid drift; read them from REF-06 when C3–5 production begins.

> **English Bloom rule — protect comprehension's "own-words / inference" reach.** Reading comprehension (`ENG-COMPREHENSION`, 5.1) is a **High-load** topic, so it is easy to drill mechanically. But the strand's value rises from literal (C2) → information & ideas (C3) → **inference & main-idea (C4–C5)**. Set §2.3 so that comprehension lessons reach **Understand/Analyze with an own-words/prediction component** rather than collapsing to copy-the-line recall. This is a free-thinking opportunity *inside* a High-load topic (carried as emphasis, per §4A discipline — not a PROTECTED tag).

> **Story → writing crossover (C5).** `ENG-STORY` culminates at C5 U20 ("Writing a Story") — a **FREEWRITE crossover** (Create level). Set the §2.3 mix to honour the composition/Create reach, not just comprehension.

### 2.4 Exit-Check — English forms

Follow REF-02 §2.4 (one observable check, 2–5 min, **per-student readout**, same Bloom level as §2.1). **Per-student readout ≠ one question per student.** A 12-pupil class does **not** get 12 different questions — it gets **12 readouts of the *same* check** (each child says / reads / writes / shows while the teacher circulates and observes each one). The check is identical for everyone so the readouts stay comparable across pupils and across classes (REF-02 §2.4 rule 3). A small set of **2–4 written variants** is used only to deter copying — never one-question-per-child. The author **picks the lesson's exit-check from the English Exit-Check Bank (§3.6)** so a stuck teacher always has a ready form. The 30% systemic / 3-lesson individual thresholds (D-025) apply unchanged. The Exit-Check Bank requirement is the universal D-043 rule, parallel to the §4.4 Example Bank.

### 2.6 Revision Anchor — read it off §4 (cross-chapter); within-chapter is a separate strand (v1.1, D-050)

**Two strands under REF-02 v1.3 (master D-050):**

1. **Within-chapter revision** — *new in v1.3, lives inside the Chapter Plan.* Session N's Hook revisits Session N−1's material via the §2.9 Session Map's previous row. English example: a 3-session "Let's Play with Sounds" chapter has Session 2 Hook revisit Session 1's initial-sound flashcards, and Session 3 Hook revisit both via sorting. ## §3 — The English Flex Zone & pedagogical posture (specializing REF-02 §4)

### 3.1 What an English teacher MAY / MAY NOT decide
Inherits REF-02 §4.1/§4.2 unchanged. English-specific points:
- **MAY:** choose which §3.2 Example-Bank word illustrates a sound/letter/pattern; choose a §3.3 discovery pattern; reorder Guided/Independent practice; add plain clapping syllable-rhythm or a non-musical Language Game (chain drill, Bingo, Memory, guessing — NCF-native, **no melody** — C-03).
- **MAY NOT:** pre-teach systematic phonics before the class has reached it (**rule E-1**); name a grammar construct before its NCF class (**rule E-2**); replace an oral exit-check with written-only where the objective is pronunciation/conversation; teach a NEEDS-REPLACEMENT element (a temple/festival setting, an anthem-as-reverence) as KEEP-AS-IS; (new/probationary teachers) reverse Hook ↔ Direct-Instruction.

### 3.2 English Example Bank conventions (REF-02 §4.4)
**Form for English = words / short phrases / short sentences**, minimum 3 per teaching point.
- Examples are concrete, age-banded, and drawn from the §2.2(B) vocabulary tiers where possible (so examples double as required-word practice).
- All examples F-01-clean and curation-clean; Islamic-resonant words are welcome `[Islamic-add]` (e.g. for the /m/ sound: *moon, mosque, **Madinah***; for sight-word practice: *I, am, a, **Muslim***; for a sentence pattern: *"This is my **Qur'an**." / "I help my **mother**."*).
- For living-being example words, use the **word only**, not a picture (C-05).
- The author lists the bank per teaching point in the lesson plan; the teacher picks or substitutes (substitution logged for the Subject Lead).

### 3.3 English Discovery-Pattern library (REF-02 §4.5)
Minimum 3 per lesson; concrete enough for a new teacher to run unaided. The canonical English patterns:

1. **Flashcard-led** — show prior letters/sounds/sight-words; pupils name; transition to new (Hook; alphabet/phonics/vocabulary).
2. **Object-led** — bring a real object whose name carries the target letter/sound (a *moon* card for /m/, a *book* for /b/); pupils name → sound/letter. *(No animal depiction — word only.)*
3. **Sound-sorting-led** — mixed picture/word cards; pupils sort by **initial / final / middle sound** (the phonics pattern, C2+) or "known / new."
4. **Story-led** — read a short story/fable; pupils predict the next event or name the moral (STORY, COMPREHENSION, POEM). *Reframe any neutral fable/moral through an Islamic-akhlaq lens.*
5. **Question-led** — open "why / what would happen if…" before content; pupils hypothesise (comprehension, free-thinking).
6. **Think-pair-share / paired exploration (PBL "share your thinking with your partner")** — pupils think alone, then discuss in pairs/groups, then present to the class (the NCF collaborative manner; the **free-thinking pattern on the oral side**; oracy, comprehension). *This is the English warrant for the 🟦 SPEAKING tag.*
7. **Picture-to-sentence** — pupils build sentences from a scene (SENTENCE → GUIDEDWRITE).
8. **Own-way prompt (write/say in your own way)** — open prompt; each pupil answers in their own words (the **free-thinking pattern on the writing side**; FREEWRITE).
9. **Role-reading / dialogue-reading as oneself** — for conversation/role units (C2 dialogues; C4 U5 "Little Doctor"; C5 discussion): pupils read assigned lines aloud **as themselves**, not impersonating a character (C-19 boundary).
10. **Learner-question / title-prediction** — pupils generate their own questions about a text or predict its title (the self-discovery pattern; NCF names learner-questions at C4 U4/U16 — may run lightly/orally earlier as Flex enrichment).

> **Language Games note.** NCF builds in non-musical games (chain drill, Bingo, Memory, Kim's game, guessing). They are excellent Hooks and practice — use them freely, **provided no melody/tune is introduced** (C-03) and any character-impersonation is converted to "as yourself" (C-19).

### 3.4 Free thinking within an Islamic framing — the PROTECTED competencies

*(This section operationalizes Instructions §6 for English and folds in the free-thinking findings from all five English TG reconciliations — §5.B, every class.)*

REF-19's load-bearing metric scores these topics **Low on dependency**, which would wrongly read as "safe to compress." REF-19 §4A tags them **🟦 PROTECTED**; this playbook treats them as **never the first to cut when time is short.** **English has exactly two — both on the free-thinking criterion; there is no Islamic-UC PROTECTED anchor (the sharp contrast with Bangla):**

| PROTECTED competency | Why protected | Where it lives across the classes |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversation & exploratory talk (`ENG-SPEAKING`)** | free-thinking *manner* (Instructions §6); NCF's collaborative pair/group talk + PBL | C1 oral interaction (3.1/3.3) → **C2 PBL "share your thinking with your partner" (U05 L6)** → C3 structured talk → C4 oral description + role-talk (U05) → **C5 the spoken backbone (3.4, 13 of 20 units; U05 discussion).** Protect *discussion and exploratory talk*, not just recitation. |
| **Free / creative writing & self-expression (`ENG-FREEWRITE`)** | free-thinking flagship (Instructions §6) | **Originates at C1** (own-way oral/word expression) → **C2 think-share origin (PBL U05 L6)** → C4 reflective/imaginative writing (the "Little Doctor" U5, "think of your own family" U8, "qualities of friends" U12, learner-questions U4/U16) → **C5 self-directed composition (Writing a Story U20) + aspiration (Champion Girl U14) + think-alone-first (U15).** |

**Free-thinking facets carried as *emphasis*, not full PROTECTED tags (§4A discipline):** the C2 preference/opinion language ("Do you like…? / I like…", 3.4), the C4 learner-generated questions, and the comprehension own-words/inference reach (§2.3). Encourage these genuinely; do not over-tag.

**Deeper-not-faster for the free-thinking strand:** when a class is ahead, enrich with richer prompts and more peer sharing — **do not** formalise free writing into timed exam essays early, and do not accelerate into the next class's Spine (REF-19 §1.4).

**Origin note (TG-confirmed):** the free-thinking strand is **native to NCF** English — PBL is formally defined in the TG ("student-centred… active engagement in real-world challenges"); "think alone, then share with your partner," "present your work to the class," and learner-generated questions recur from C1 to C5. Emphasising it aligns the school's mission *with* NCF intent.

### 3.5 Redirection patterns (the discovery-stays-Islamic move)
REF-02 §4.3 defers redirection patterns to REF-03. When a pupil's hypothesis, example, or own-way answer diverges from Islamic values, the teacher redirects **without shaming**:
- *Pupil offers a song/jingle/tune as a memory aid* → "Lovely! Now let's say it together **without the tune**, in a clear chant" — keep the rhythm, drop the melody (C-03).
- *Pupil names a Western pop figure / cartoon character as an example* → "Good try! Let's use a name **we know** — say a Muslim friend's name" — redirect to a local/Muslim name (C-10/F-01).
- *Pupil's own-way story drifts into a festival / temple / idolatry frame* (echoing the C4 U16–17 content) → acknowledge the effort, **redirect the setting** to a permissible one (Eid, family, a halal trip, nature as Allah's signs) while keeping the child's idea (C-01/C-04).
- *Pupil personifies the sun/rain/nature as the giver of life* (Gardening, Environment, Sundarbans texts) → "**Who** sends the rain and makes plants grow?" — re-anchor causation in Allah (C-09). *(The main delivery-side alignment move in English.)*
- *Pupil wants to "be" a character / act it out* → redirect to **role-reading as oneself** or genuine practice (C-19).
The redirection preserves the child's initiative (the free-thinking value) and corrects only the framing. These patterns also seed Project 07 teacher training.

### 3.6 English Exit-Check Bank (ready forms for §2.4)

*A pre-prepared inventory of exit-check forms — the §2.4 counterpart of the §3.2 Example Bank (D-043). The author supplies **≥2 ready forms per lesson's objective type** so a stuck teacher always has a hint to pick from; the teacher runs the chosen form as a **per-student readout** (the same check observed for each child — §2.4), not as one question per child.*

| Objective type | Ready exit-check forms (pick one; run per-student) | Readout the teacher confirms |
|---|---|---|
| Letter / sound (`ALPHABET`) | "Show me this letter's card" · "Make this sound" · pick the letter from 3 cards | each child points / sounds individually |
| Phonics — initial/final/middle sound (`PHONICS`) | "Say a word that **starts / ends** with this sound" · sort 3 picture-cards by sound | each child's spoken word / sort |
| Sight / content word (`VOCAB`) | "Read this word" · "Use this word in a short spoken phrase" | each child reads / uses the word |
| Sentence (`SENTENCE`) | "Read this sentence aloud" · "Look at the picture and write/say one small sentence" | each child reads / writes one sentence |
| Read-aloud (`READALOUD`) | "Read this line aloud **with stress**" (C4+) | each child's read-aloud turn |
| Listening 🟦 (`LISTENING`/`SPEAKING`) | "Tell me **in your own words** what I just said" · pair: each tells partner, then teacher | each child's oral response |
| Conversation 🟦 (`SPEAKING`) | "Ask your partner one question, then tell me their answer" · "Describe this in two sentences" | each child's spoken turn (assess **participation + on-topic**, not perfection) |
| Rhyme / poem (`RHYME`/`POEM`) | "Recite the first two lines" | individual recitation |
| Story / comprehension (`COMPREHENSION`/`STORY`) | one targeted "what / why" per child · or "Tell me in one sentence, **your own way**, what happened" | each child's answer (own-way doubles as a free-think check) |

**Rules.** (1) Same check for all pupils → comparable readouts; never one-question-per-child. (2) 2–4 written variants only to deter copying. (3) The form sits at the §2.1/§2.3 Bloom level. (4) F-01 and the §2.5 cross-walk apply to any text in the check. (5) The bank is a *floor* for the stuck teacher — an experienced teacher may substitute an equivalent per-student check.

## §5 — Per-class emphasis digest (consuming REF-19 v1.9)

This is a **digest**, not a re-print — REF-19 v1.9 remains the source of truth for the 23-topic English map, the load-bearing tiers, and the §4A PROTECTED tags. For each class it answers the lesson-author's two questions: *what must be mastered this year (load-bearing)?* and *what must be protected this year regardless of structural weight (PROTECTED)?* — under the "deeper-not-faster" guardrail (REF-19 §1.4: ahead-of-pace classes go deeper in the same topic; never accelerate into the next class's Spine).

**Cross-class vertical anchors (emphasise the through-line, not just the year):**
- **The communicative spine** (alphabet → vocabulary → sentence → comprehension, with speaking running alongside) is the High-load backbone; its early rungs (C1 alphabet, C2 systematic phonics) are the highest-value mastery checkpoints in the whole subject.
- **The critical handoff** is **ALPHABET (C1) → systematic PHONICS (C2)** — systematic phonics is **Era-B-new at C2** with no C1 antecedent (rule E-1); confirm C1 letter-sound mastery before the C2 step.
- **The 7.3 form-fill anchor** is a clean **C1 → C4 → C5 vertical anchor** (C1 name/info form → C4 U13 event/online registration → C5 U06 email) — the English counterpart of Bangla's ১৪.৩; treat C1's form-fill as the foundation, not a throwaway.
- **Free thinking runs C1 → C5 continuously**: own-way oral (C1) → think-pair-share PBL (C2) → reflective/imaginative writing + learner-questions (C4) → self-directed composition (C5). It is the school's flagship free-thinking competency and is PROTECTED at every class.
- **There is NO Islamic-Universal-Core PROTECTED anchor in English.** Islamic alignment is carried through **Project 05 replacement** (the C4 U16–17 festival/temple cluster, song framings, illustrations) and through **delivery** (adab/akhlaq in commands/requests; signs-of-Allah framing in nature/info texts). The one Islam-positive source element is the **C4 U3 holy-book recitation** — foreground it.

### 5.1 — Class 1 emphasis
**Master (High):** alphabet & letter-sounds (the full-acquisition year — rock-solid foundation), the 30-word Universal Core, sentence templates, conversation/speaking. These are the foundation the entire stack rests on. **Protect (🟦):** speaking (the collaborative/oral manner) and **free/own-way expression — which *originates* at C1** (oral own-way), not at C2. **Deeper-not-faster:** an advanced C1 class enriches *within* C1 — cleaner handwriting, more word-building, oral blending, richer own-way prompts — and **must not start systematic phonics** (that is C2, rule E-1). **Curation watch:** the "good morning song" (U1) and rhyme delivery → recitation, not melody (C-03); review illustrations (C-05).
