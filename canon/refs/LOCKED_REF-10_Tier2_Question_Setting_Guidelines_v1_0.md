# Tier 2 Question-Setting Guidelines

**REF-10 · v1.0 · the higher (stretch / scholarship) standard, built on Tier 1 (REF-09)**

**Purpose.** The higher standard for the school's *stretch* question-writing — বৃত্তি (primary scholarship), Olympiad-style enrichment, admission preparation for strong high schools / cadet colleges / top madaris, and gifted enrichment for the school's strongest students. Tier 2 is the **ceiling** that pulls the top of the class upward. It does not loosen anything below it: **Tier 1 (REF-09) remains the floor under every question in the school.**

**Tier 2 builds on Tier 1 — it does not replace it.** Everything in REF-09 still binds: the seven musts, alignment, curation, vocabulary, the mandatory key/rubric, and the review gate. This document adds **only the higher-standard delta.** Read REF-09 first; use **REF-17 (V1B)** / **REF-18 (V1C)** for all Bloom's mechanics (levels, verbs, stems, blueprint, MCQ and creative construction). REF-10 restates neither.

**Who Tier 2 binds — and who it does not.** Tier 2 does **not** bind every teacher, and it does **not** govern the regular class test or the half-yearly / annual exam — those are Tier 1's domain. Tier 2 governs only the subset of question-writing aimed at the four stretch purposes above, **authored by Subject Leads and designated stretch-authors** and reviewed under the stricter §6 gate. A student is ready for Tier 2 work only once their Tier 1 foundation is solid — you cannot stretch a foundation that is not yet built.

**What Tier 2 owns (the delta over Tier 1).**
- The **Tier 2 question profile** — non-routine, multi-part, transfer-demanding, high-Bloom.
- **Upward difficulty** plus a second stretch dimension — **novelty / transfer**.
- **Exam-pattern alignment** — Britti, Olympiad, and admission shapes, adapted to the school.
- **Sophisticated distractors** and **full model solutions** (stricter than Tier 1's key).
- The **Tier 2 Islamic reasoning guardrail** — how to push high-order reasoning without crossing into matters of the deen.
- The **stricter Tier 2 Review Gate** and the **strong-student enrichment bench**.

**Authority note.** As in REF-09: Bloom bands defer to **REF-06 §3.6**; chapter/session scope to **REF-02 v1.3**; curation to **REF-01**; names to **REF-20**; English vocabulary to **REF-22**; the Bloom mechanics to **REF-17 / REF-18**; and the **floor to REF-09**. Tier 2 sets only the higher standard.

## §1 — What Tier 2 Is, Who It Binds, Where It Sits

Tier 2 exists to serve the strongest students well — the ones who clear Tier 1 easily and would stagnate on a diet of foundation work. Its four purposes:
- **বৃত্তি / primary scholarship** — the competitive, time-pressured, curriculum-based scholarship pattern (Class 5): mostly higher-application items under time, rewarding speed *and* accuracy.
- **Olympiad-style enrichment** (math / science) — non-routine problem solving where no formula is handed over; the challenge is to *find* the path.
- **Admission preparation** — for strong high schools, cadet colleges, and top madaris: English, mathematics, general knowledge, and reasoning, with Arabic / Islamic GK an added strength for our students.
- **Gifted / stretch enrichment** generally — keeping able students growing.

**Where it sits.** Tier 2 is an **enrichment track running alongside** the regular Tier 1 class work — supplementary material for strong students, not the standard paper. A normal exam stays Tier 1 (with its easy floor intact so weak students pass); Tier 2 items live in enrichment sets, scholarship practice, and the stretch slots of a paper, clearly marked.

**Who authors it.** Subject Leads and teachers they designate as stretch-authors. Tier 2 question-writing is harder than Tier 1 and is reviewed more strictly (§6); it is not part of every teacher's daily duty.

## §2 — The Tier 2 Question Profile (the four marks of a stretch question)

A Tier 2 question is recognised by four marks. A strong stretch item shows most of them; a "hard" item showing none of them is just Tier 1 difficulty, not Tier 2.

1. **Non-routine.** No single taught procedure solves it directly. The student must *choose* a method, *combine* methods, or *see* a structure that is not announced. (Tier 1 "Apply" uses a known method; Tier 2 makes the student decide *which* method, or *that one is needed*.)
2. **Multi-part and climbing.** Built in parts that escalate through the levels within one question (REF-17 §7.3), pushed further — the later parts genuinely require the earlier ones.
3. **Transfer.** A known idea is used in an **unfamiliar context** — same concept, new clothing — so memorised worked-examples do not carry the student through.
4. **High-Bloom concentrated.** Dominant in Apply / Analyze / Evaluate / Create; Remember appears only as the entry rung, not the body.

**Contrast — Class 5 Math.** *Tier 1 (Apply):* "Find the area of a 6 m × 4 m room." *Tier 2 (non-routine + transfer):* "A 6 m × 4 m prayer room is to be covered with square mats. Mats come only in 1 m and 2 m sizes and the 2 m mats are cheaper per area. What is the fewest-cost way to cover the floor with no gaps, and why?" — same area concept, but the student must reason about tiling, cost, and constraint.

**Contrast — Class 5 Science.** *Tier 1 (Understand):* "Why do wet clothes dry faster in sun?" *Tier 2 (transfer + evaluate):* "Two identical wet cloths are hung — one in shade with wind, one in still sunlight. Predict which dries first, and justify using more than one factor." — the student must weigh competing causes, not recall one.

Keep all of it **curriculum-true, curation-clean, and within the class's taught content** — stretch the thinking, never the syllabus.

## §3 — Upward Difficulty + the Novelty / Transfer Dimension

Tier 2 changes the difficulty picture in two ways.

**(a) The Tier 1 easy floor relaxes.** Because the audience is strong students, the easy ≥ 30% floor (REF-09 §3) does **not** apply to a Tier 2 enrichment set — those sets weight **medium and hard** and may open with a medium item rather than an easy one. (When a Tier 2 item sits *inside* an otherwise Tier 1 paper, the paper as a whole still keeps its Tier 1 floor — the Tier 2 item is the stretch slot, not the whole paper.)

**(b) A second axis — novelty / transfer.** Tier 1 calibrates one stretch axis (easy / medium / hard, REF-09 §3). Tier 2 adds a second:

| Novelty band | What it asks of the student |
| --- | --- |
| **Familiar** | the taught context, harder numbers (this is really Tier 1 hard) |
| **Adapted** | the taught idea in a *changed* context (the Tier 2 working range) |
| **Novel** | a context the student has not seen, solvable only by transferring a principle |

A Tier 2 set lives mostly in **Adapted**, reaching **Novel** for the top end. Set both axes on purpose: a question can be *Adapted + medium* (gentle stretch) or *Novel + hard* (Olympiad-grade).

**The discipline that separates Tier 2 from "just hard."** Difficulty must come from **reasoning depth, transfer, or synthesis** — never from un-taught trivia, obscure facts, or trick wording. A question that is hard only because it uses a word the class never learned, or hides the real task in confusing language, is a **bad** question at any tier (REF-17 §11). Tier 2 is hard *honestly*.

## §4 — Exam-Pattern Alignment (Britti / Olympiad / Admission)

Tier 2 prepares students for real external shapes. Borrow the **shape**, never an actual question carrying flagged content — adapt to curriculum-true, curation-clean, Islamically-framed material (§7).

- **বৃত্তি / primary scholarship (Class 5).** Curriculum-based but at higher application, under time pressure, MCQ-heavy with some written. *Tier 2 prep:* curriculum-true items at scholarship difficulty, plus **timed practice** so accuracy holds at speed. Distractors must be of scholarship quality (§6).
- **Olympiad-style (Math / Science).** Non-routine, no handed-over method; the §2 marks are the whole point. Keep **age-appropriate** — a Class 4 Olympiad item is simple in content, deep in reasoning, not advanced in syllabus.
- **Admission (strong high schools / cadet / top madaris).** Typically English + mathematics + general knowledge + reasoning. Our students gain by adding **Arabic and Islamic general knowledge** strength. *Tier 2 prep:* mixed-skill sets that mirror the admission balance, all within taught content.

In every case the rule from §2–§3 holds: stretch the **thinking and the time-discipline**, keep the **syllabus** to what was taught, and keep the **content** curation-clean.

## §5 — The Tier 2 Islamic Reasoning Guardrail

Tier 2 lives at the high-order levels — Evaluate, judge, critique, justify, design. That is exactly where free thinking flowers, and exactly where it must be **channelled**, because a careless "critique" or "form your own ruling" prompt at this level could invite a child to sit in judgement over the deen. This guardrail is Tier 2's most important addition to REF-09 §7 / REF-17 §9.

**The rules:**
1. **Aim higher-order reasoning at academic, dunya, and adab content** — mathematical methods, scientific claims, citizenship, a story character's choice, a practical plan. **Never** at settled matters of aqeedah, established rulings, or the revealed texts.
2. **"Evaluate" inside Islam means apply revealed criteria to a case**, not judge the teaching itself. *Good:* "Evaluate Karim's action in light of Islamic honesty." *Not:* "Do you think the Islamic rule on honesty is correct?"
3. **Avoid speculative or fitna-prone prompts** — sectarian disputes, contested theology, "what if" questions about the unseen. Tier 2 rigor is spent on **beneficial knowledge (ʿilm nāfiʿ)**, **tafakkur in Allah's creation**, and sound reasoning, consistent with the school's Salafi framing (Project 00 §7; REF-12).

| Unsafe Tier 2 stretch prompt | Better Tier 2 framing |
| --- | --- |
| "Critique the ruling on zakat." | "A shopkeeper's wealth and debts are given — analyse whether zakat is due and calculate it, justifying each step." |
| "Form your own opinion on whether salah is necessary." | "Evaluate how a consistent salah routine could solve a described student's time-management problem, with reasons." |
| "Debate which sect is right." | "Compare two halal business plans and judge which better serves the community, using Islamic and practical criteria." |

Properly channelled, Tier 2 produces students who reason rigorously *and* keep adab with the intellect — the Qur'anic call to تفكر and تعقل, aimed where reasoning belongs.

## §6 — The Tier 2 Review Gate + Reviewer Checklist

Tier 2 items are reviewed by a **Subject Lead** — always, with no exceptions — and the gate is stricter than Tier 1's. The Tier 1 §9 checklist still applies in full; Tier 2 **adds** the checks below. Submit with a **full model solution**, not just a key.

| # | Tier 2 added check | Pass? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Genuinely non-routine** — not hard recall or a long Tier 1 item in disguise (§2). | ☐ |
| 2 | **Curriculum-anchored** — stretches thinking, not syllabus; nothing un-taught. | ☐ |
| 3 | **Difficulty is honest** — from reasoning/transfer/synthesis, not trivia or trickery (§3). | ☐ |
| 4 | **Solvable & reachable** — a full model solution exists and a prepared strong student can reach it. | ☐ |
| 5 | **Time-realistic** for the target pattern (Britti speed, Olympiad depth, admission balance) (§4). | ☐ |
| 6 | **Passes the Islamic reasoning guardrail** (§5) — high-order reasoning aimed where it belongs. | ☐ |
| 7 | **Full model solution attached** (worked, with the reasoning shown), plus a rubric for any open part. | ☐ |
| 8 | All **Tier 1 musts** still hold (curation-clean, vocabulary-clean, level/difficulty-labelled — REF-09). | ☐ |

A "No" on any line sends the item back with the same respectful, specific feedback Tier 1 uses. A Tier 2 item is **not used** until the gate passes.

## §7 — Building and Sourcing the Enrichment Bench

**How Tier 2 items are made.** Borrow the *shape* of strong external questions (past Britti, Olympiad, admission, good textbooks) and **re-skin** them onto curriculum-true, curation-clean, Islamically-framed content — the upward cousin of REF-09 §6's repetition-with-variation. Never lift a question whole if it carries flagged names, contexts, or content (REF-01); rebuild the context from approved material (Project 00 §7; REF-20).

**Where the stretch is spent.** Concentrate Tier 2 work on the **load-bearing spine topics** (REF-19 / REF-03 🔴 High) — depth on the topics future years depend on returns the most. Terminal / low topics rarely need a Tier 2 bench.

**The enrichment Pool.** Tier 2 items collect in an **enrichment Pool**, tagged for strong students and kept separate from the Tier 1 homework Pool (REF-08), each with its **full model solution**. This Pool feeds scholarship practice, Olympiad clubs, and the stretch slots of papers, and is the school's growing Tier 2 asset (Project 04).

## §8 — The Tier 2 Setting Workflow (stepwise)

1. **Confirm readiness & target.** The topic's Tier 1 foundation is solid; name the target pattern (Britti / Olympiad / admission / general stretch) and the curriculum outcome it anchors to.
2. **Design for non-routineness.** Choose the §2 marks to build in — which method must be *chosen* or *combined*, what unfamiliar context carries the transfer.
3. **Set both stretch axes.** Difficulty (REF-09 §3) **and** novelty/transfer (§3 here); place the item in the Adapted–Novel range.
4. **Write — curriculum-true, curation-clean, vocabulary-clean** (REF-09 §7), using REF-17 §7.2/§7.3 for MCQ and multi-part construction.
5. **Write the full model solution now** — worked, reasoning shown; a rubric for any open part. If you cannot solve it cleanly, the question is broken — fix it.
6. **Guardrail check.** Run §5 — high-order reasoning aimed at academic / dunya / adab content, never at the deen.
7. **Time-test.** Solve it under the target pattern's time; if a prepared strong student cannot finish in realistic time, adjust.
8. **Self-check, then the Subject-Lead Tier 2 gate (§6).** Not used until the gate passes; file the item with its solution into the enrichment Pool (§7).

---

## §9 — Ready-Check (before any Tier 2 item is submitted)

- ☐ The topic's **Tier 1 foundation is solid**; the **target pattern** is named.
- ☐ The item is **genuinely non-routine** and **curriculum-anchored** (thinking stretched, syllabus not).
- ☐ Both stretch axes set on purpose; difficulty is **honest** (reasoning/transfer, not trivia/trickery).
- ☐ A **full model solution** is written and **reachable** in realistic time.
- ☐ The **§5 Islamic guardrail** passes — reasoning aimed where it belongs.
- ☐ All **Tier 1 musts** still hold (curation, vocabulary, labels, key/rubric — REF-09).
- ☐ Ready for the **stricter Subject-Lead Tier 2 gate**.
