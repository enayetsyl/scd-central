## §1 — The Six Levels at a Glance

| Level | Bangla | Student's mental work | Question asks… | One-line line *above the level below* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Remember** | মনে রাখা | Recall / recognise facts, terms, rules, formulas, texts | "Can the student bring it back from memory?" | Recall **without looking** — not copying from the page. |
| **Understand** | বোঝা | Explain, summarise, classify, give examples, restate in own words | "Does the student know what this means?" | Explains the meaning — does not just **repeat** the wording. |
| **Apply** | প্রয়োগ | Use a learned rule / formula / method in a situation | "Can the student use what they learned?" | **Uses** the rule — does not just **explain** it. |
| **Analyze** | বিশ্লেষণ | Break into parts; compare; find cause, pattern, relationship | "Can the student see the structure / cause / pattern?" | **Examines** the situation first — not a single fixed procedure (Apply). |
| **Evaluate** | মূল্যায়ন | Judge using criteria, evidence, reasons | "Can the student make a reasoned judgment?" | **Judges with criteria** — Analyze only laid the ground. |
| **Create** | সৃষ্টি | Produce something new by combining knowledge + judgment | "Can the student make something meaningful and original?" | **Produces** a new response — Evaluate only judged an existing one. |

Remember is the foundation (no level below it). Higher is **not** automatically better — pick the level that fits the class, subject, and purpose (see §6).

## §4 — Class Targets

### 4.1 Dominant levels by class
| Class | Dominant levels | Suitable higher-level exposure | Practical note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Remember, Understand | simple Apply, simple Create | pictures, objects, oral answers, matching, short sentences |
| 2 | Remember, Understand, simple Apply | simple Analyze (grouping) | classify, match, use words, simple story sums |
| 3 | Understand, Apply | simple Analyze, guided Create | begin "why / how / compare" on familiar content |
| 4 | Understand, Apply | Analyze, simple Evaluate | tables, diagrams, short explanations, real-life problems |
| 5 | Apply, Understand, Analyze | Evaluate, guided Create | scholarship-style structured reasoning |

*(Bands for Classes 6–10 are in REF-06 §3.2; the school currently runs to Class 5.)*

### 4.2 Indicative % mix — **chapter scope**
| Class group | Remember | Understand | Apply | Analyze | Evaluate | Create |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Class 1–2 | 35–45% | 30–40% | 15–25% | 0–10% | 0–5% | 0–5% |
| Class 3–5 | 20–30% | 25–35% | 25–35% | 10–20% | 0–10% | 0–10% |

> **Chapter, not session (D-050).** These bands describe the **whole chapter**, however many periods it spans. Across a multi-period chapter the mix *climbs*: early sessions sit at the chapter's lower end (Remember / Understand-leaning), later sessions reach into Apply / Analyze as the band allows. A single-period chapter collapses to one session and the band is read directly. The per-session focus lives in the Session Map (REF-02 §2.9). These are **indicative** — exact mark splits come later from the Tier 1 / Tier 2 question-setting guidelines.

**Age rule both ways:** do not *under-demand* older students (definition-only questions weaken them); do not *over-demand* younger ones (keep the language and context simple — a Class 1 child *can* evaluate: "Was it good that Ayesha helped her friend? Why?").

## §5 — Free Thinking + Islamic Framing

Free thinking (স্বাধীন চিন্তা) is not careless thinking — it is observing, asking, comparing, reasoning, justifying, and expressing **with responsibility**. Analyze / Evaluate / Create are the levels that open it: they move the student past one memorised line.

**Open-ended questions** invite more than one valid answer and must still be marked fairly. Use a criteria rubric (10-mark shape):

| Criteria | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| Content accuracy | 3 |
| Reasoning (reasons + evidence) | 3 |
| Organization | 2 |
| Islamic / value alignment | 2 |
| **Total** | **10** |

**Frame thinking *inside* revealed guidance** — adab with the intellect, not against it. Open the door to the student's reasoning, but anchor it:

| Unsafe framing | Better Islamic framing |
| --- | --- |
| "Say anything you want about salah." | "Explain how salah can build discipline in a student's life, using examples." |
| "Make your own rule about halal and haram." | "Explain what a Muslim should check before eating packaged food, using what you learned." |
| "Give any opinion about honesty." | "Evaluate the student's action in light of Islamic honesty and school rules." |

Free thinking and Islamic adab are not enemies — properly framed, each strengthens the other.

## §6 — Watch-Outs (the five that mis-level a question)

1. **High-level verb ≠ high-level question.** "Analyze" in the stem means nothing if the task only needs recall. The *mental work* sets the level, not the verb.
2. **"Creative" ≠ automatically high-Bloom's.** A drawing or "make your own" task is Create only if it requires meaningful organisation or evidence of learning — otherwise it is disguised memorisation or a craft task.
3. **Listing causes is Remember**, not Analyze — *unless* the student must classify, compare, or explain relationships among them.
4. **Copying is not Remember.** Recall means *without looking*; "copy from page 15" assesses handwriting, not memory.
5. **Difficulty ≠ level.** A question can be hard and still Remember (an obscure fact), or simple and still Evaluate (an age-appropriate "why is this better?"). Match the level to purpose, then set difficulty separately.

## §7 — Ready-Check (tick before you finalise)

- ☐ I named the **level I intend** for each question / teaching point.
- ☐ The **mental work** matches that level (ran the §6 watch-outs).
- ☐ The set fits the **class band** (§4.1) and sits roughly within the **chapter % mix** (§4.2).
- ☐ Across a multi-period chapter, the levels **climb** (lower early, higher later).
- ☐ Any open-ended item has a **criteria rubric** and **Islamic framing** (§5).
- ☐ Memorisation is used as a **foundation**, not the whole paper.
