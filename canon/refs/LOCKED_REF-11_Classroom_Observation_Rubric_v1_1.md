## §0 — Summary and how-to-use checklist (read this first)

**What this document is.** A single, standardized sheet an observer (the Principal or a Subject Lead) uses to watch one teaching **session** (one period) and record, fairly and consistently, how well it met the school's teaching standard. It scores **five quality domains** on a 4-level growth scale and applies **two pass/fail gates** that no amount of polish can override.

**The one-sentence rule.** *Score the five domains for quality; check the two gates for breaches; debrief for growth — never to shame.*

**The instrument at a glance**

| Part | What | How recorded |
|---|---|---|
| **5 scored domains** | D1 Structure · D2 Cognitive demand · D3 Free thinking · D4 Values & adab · D5 Engagement & feedback | 1–4 level each (3 = the working standard) |
| **2 gates** | G1 Curation fidelity · G2 Materials & safety | PASS / BREACH (a breach is flagged regardless of scores) |
| **Debrief** | One strength + one growth focus, agreed with the teacher | Written on the sheet; one focus carries to next time |

**The 4-level scale (growth-framed).**

| Level | Bangla | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | এখনও নয় (Not yet) | The practice is not evident this session. |
| **2** | বিকাশমান (Emerging) | Present in part / inconsistent — visibly developing. |
| **3** | নির্ভরযোগ্য (Secure) | **The working standard.** Reliably present and effective. |
| **4** | শক্তিশালী (Strong) | Exemplary — a model others could learn from. |

> **3 is the target, not 4.** Early in the initiative many sessions will sit at **2 (Emerging)** — that is normal and expected, not failure. The rubric measures growth over time, not a one-day verdict. A teacher who moves a domain from 2 → 3 across a term has succeeded.

**Observer's pre-flight checklist (tick before you review the footage):**

- [ ] I have the teacher's **Chapter Plan + this session's Session Plan** (REF-02) in hand.
- [ ] I know the **class** and have its **Bloom band** open (§9).
- [ ] I have a **blank §5 sheet** (one per session).
- [ ] I have told the teacher **which session I'm reviewing** and that a **growth debrief will follow** (§6.1).
- [ ] I have **REF-18 (Bloom pocket)** and the **curation quick-list** (§4.1) to hand for the two technical checks.

**Observer's post-session checklist (tick before you leave the debrief):**

- [ ] Five domains scored 1–4, with **one concrete note** per domain.
- [ ] Two gates marked PASS / BREACH (any breach written up specifically).
- [ ] **One genuine strength** named first.
- [ ] **One growth focus** agreed (not five — one).
- [ ] Sheet signed/dated; copy to the teacher; the growth focus logged for next observation.

## §1 — Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
REF-11 exists so that *every* observer judges teaching against *the same standard, in the same words.* Without it, two Subject Leads watching the same lesson would score it differently and give contradictory advice. The rubric turns the school's teaching commitments — the three-layer lesson model (REF-02), the Bloom's climb (REF-17/18), free thinking within tawhid (REF-12 §4 / Project 00 Instructions §6), Islamic adab (REF-12 §5–§8), and curation fidelity (REF-01/21) — into a small set of observable, scoreable behaviours, plus a debrief that grows the teacher rather than grading them.

### 1.2 What REF-11 is
- A **session-level** instrument: it observes **one teaching period**, because that is what an observer can actually see end-to-end. (Across several observations of the same teacher, the picture becomes a chapter- and term-level one.)
- A **developmental** tool: the 4-level scale is a growth ladder, and the debrief carries exactly **one** growth focus forward.
- A **consistency** tool: anyone trained on it should score the same session within one level of any other trained observer.

### 1.3 What REF-11 is *not*
- It is **not** a teacher appraisal, pay, or disciplinary instrument. Employment matters live in the School Handbook, not here. Mixing the two destroys the trust the debrief depends on.
- It is **not** a lesson-plan checker. Whether the *plan* is well-formed is REF-02's reading-rule lists (§1 of REF-02). REF-11 checks whether the *teaching* delivered it.
- It is **not** the curation policy or the Bloom standard. It *applies* REF-01/21 and REF-17/18; where it and they differ, **they govern** and REF-11 is corrected.

### 1.4 Who observes
The **Principal** and trained **Subject Leads**. A teacher should be observed by someone who knows the subject well enough to judge cognitive demand and curation in it. Self-observation (a teacher scoring their own recorded session) and peer observation are encouraged as practice but do not replace a Lead/Principal observation in the formal cycle (§8).

## §2 — How the instrument works

### 2.1 Domains are scored; gates are checked
The **five domains** (§3) ask *how good was the teaching?* — a quality continuum, scored 1–4. The **two gates** (§4) ask *did anything cross a hard line?* — curation and materials are not "more or less good," they are kept or breached. A gate breach is recorded **on its own** and addressed in the debrief **regardless of how high the domain scores are.** A polished, engaging lesson that reintroduced a sung tune (curation breach) or ran in an unsafe, disorderly room (materials breach) is still a breach: you do not average it away.

### 2.2 Reading the score
- There is **no total or average.** A single number hides where to help. The output is the **profile** — five domain levels + two gate results + one growth focus.
- **3 (Secure) is the standard.** Read the profile as: which domains are at 3+, which are at 2 (the natural place to coach next), which (rarely, and worth attention) are at 1.
- **Trend beats snapshot.** The same domain observed across three sessions tells you far more than one session. The trackers (§8) hold the trend; the sheet holds the snapshot.

### 2.3 One focus at a time
The debrief names **one** growth focus, not a list. A teacher given five things to fix changes nothing; a teacher given one clear, achievable next step changes one thing — and that is how the standard rises. This mirrors the school's own pedagogy: you scaffold one rung at a time.

## §3 — The five scored domains

Each domain lists **what to look for** (the observable indicators), then anchors the four levels. Score the domain as a whole from the anchors; use the indicators as evidence. Write **one concrete note** per domain (what you actually saw), never just the number.

### D1 — Lesson Structure & Pacing · পাঠ-কাঠামো ও গতি
*(grounded in REF-02 v1.6 — the Session Plan's mandatory rituals and the Lesson Flow)*

**Look for:**
- **Opening (সূচনা):** room settled; the session begins with the name of Allah / blessing on learning (REF-02 §3.1).
- A **clear session objective** that matches the Session Map row (the teacher and the children know what this period is for).
- **Hook (আকর্ষণ):** connects to the prior session or to lived experience before new content.
- The **must-cover slice** for this session is actually delivered, in a sensible order, at a pace the class can follow (not rushed, not stalled).
- **Exit-Check (নির্গমন পরীক্ষা):** a real check is run near the end and the teacher *uses* it to spot who is struggling (REF-02 §2.4).
- **Closing (সমাপ্তি):** the lesson is closed properly — materials secured, gratitude/du'a invoked.

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Mandatory rituals largely absent (no clear opening/closing, no exit-check); the session drifts or has no visible objective. |
| 2 | Some rituals present but inconsistent; objective vague; exit-check skipped or not used; pacing uneven (rushed end or long stall). |
| 3 | **Opening, Hook, delivery of the must-cover slice, Exit-Check, and Closing all present and purposeful; objective clear; pacing fits the class.** |
| 4 | All of 3, plus the structure is fluid and confident; the exit-check genuinely redirects the last minutes toward pupils who needed it. |

### D2 — Cognitive Demand & Bloom's Climb · চিন্তার গভীরতা
*(grounded in REF-17 / REF-18 — the class band and the climb)*

**Look for:**
- Questioning **goes beyond recall** where the class band allows (§9): the children explain, apply, compare, or judge — not only name and list.
- The level is **appropriate to the class** — neither under-demanding older pupils with definition-only questions nor over-demanding younger ones (REF-18 §4.2 "age rule both ways").
- Demand **climbs** within the session (and across the chapter): early minutes can sit at Remember/Understand; later minutes reach into Apply/Analyze as the band permits.
- The teacher's stems match the level they intend (REF-18 §6: a high-level verb alone is not a high-level question).

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Almost entirely recall/copying; questions sit below the class band; no climb. |
| 2 | Mostly recall with occasional "why/how," but the higher questions are unplanned or mis-levelled; demand is flat. |
| 3 | **A deliberate mix that fits the class band; genuine Understand/Apply (and some Analyze for C3–5) appears; demand climbs across the session.** |
| 4 | All of 3, plus well-pitched Analyze/Evaluate (or guided Create) that the children can actually reach; questioning adapts live to their answers. |

### D3 — Free Thinking & Self-Discovery within tawhid · স্বাধীন চিন্তা ও স্ব-অন্বেষণ
*(grounded in Project 00 Instructions §6 + REF-12 §4 + REF-18 §5)*

**Look for:**
- Pupils get to **try first** — predict, attempt, estimate, or reason aloud — *before* being told the answer.
- Questions and tasks that **invite more than one valid response** (within Islamic guidance), not one fixed expected word.
- **Productive struggle is allowed:** the teacher waits, prompts, and lets children reach it, rather than rescuing instantly.
- The teacher acts as a **facilitator of reasoning**, not only a transmitter of facts; "what do you think, and why?" is a real part of the room.
- Inquiry is **anchored in revealed guidance** — open-ended, but framed inside the Deen (REF-18 §5: free thinking and adab strengthen each other), e.g. wonder at the signs of Allah in nature rather than open-ended speculation against tawhid.

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pure transmission: children receive and repeat; no try-first, no open question, struggle is shut down. |
| 2 | Occasional open question, but the teacher answers it themselves quickly; try-first is rare; reasoning is not asked for. |
| 3 | **Children regularly try first and explain their reasoning; at least one genuine open-ended, Islamically-framed prompt is used and pupils' own thinking is drawn out.** |
| 4 | All of 3, plus a culture of inquiry is visible — children ask their own questions, justify with evidence, and the teacher routinely turns "telling" into "discovering," all within tawhid. |

### D4 — Islamic Values & Adab in the Classroom · ইসলামি মূল্যবোধ ও আদব
*(grounded in REF-12 §5–§8 — the school's core values and conduct)*

**Look for:**
- **Adab and akhlaq** are modeled and reinforced: respect (especially toward the teacher and among pupils), honesty, gratitude, contentment, modesty.
- The teacher's **own conduct** reflects ihsan — patience, fairness, dignity; effort and character valued over marks alone (becoming a Muhsin, REF-12 §5).
- **Positive Islamic framing** is woven naturally where the content allows (signs of Allah, prophetic example, halal/adab) — not forced, but present.
- The classroom **upholds modesty and the values charter** in how children are addressed, grouped, and corrected.

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Conduct or content works against the values (harshness, humiliation, marks-only framing, values ignored where clearly relevant). |
| 2 | Values neutral — nothing against them, but adab/akhlaq are not actively reinforced and openings to frame positively are missed. |
| 3 | **Adab and akhlaq are actively modeled and reinforced; the teacher's conduct reflects ihsan; positive Islamic framing appears where natural.** |
| 4 | All of 3, plus the values are a visible, lived ethos of the room — children's own adab is evident and the teacher draws value-lessons gracefully from the content. |

### D5 — Engagement, Feedback & Management · অংশগ্রহণ, ফিডব্যাক ও শ্রেণি-ব্যবস্থাপনা
*(grounded in REF-07/08 for the assessment/homework checks; general classroom practice for the rest)*

**Look for:**
- **All** pupils are engaged and on-task — not only the front row or the eager few; quieter children are drawn in.
- **Feedback is specific and encouraging** — it tells a child what was good and what to do next, without harshness or sarcasm (this protects the child and models the school's adab).
- The teacher **checks understanding** during the lesson (not only at the exit-check) and adjusts.
- **Homework / revision**, if set this session, is within the budget and tagged to its topic (REF-08 cap; REF-07 `TOP-…` tag); any spaced-revision touch due is honoured.
- The room is **orderly and safe** and managed **without harshness**.

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Few engaged; feedback absent or harsh; no live checking; disorder, or order kept by fear. |
| 2 | Partial engagement (the same few); feedback thin or only corrective; some checking; management functional but rigid or occasionally harsh. |
| 3 | **Most pupils engaged including quieter ones; feedback is specific and encouraging; understanding is checked and acted on; the room is orderly without harshness; homework/revision handled per REF-07/08.** |
| 4 | All of 3, plus near-universal engagement; feedback visibly moves learning forward; management is warm and near-invisible. |

## §4 — The two gates (PASS / BREACH)

Gates are **binary** and **non-negotiable.** Mark each PASS or BREACH. A BREACH is written up specifically (what, where) and is the **first** item in the debrief, ahead of any domain coaching — but it is addressed as a *correction to make*, not a character judgment.

### G1 — Curation Fidelity · কিউরেশন সততা
*(applies REF-01 v1.2 + REF-21 to live delivery — does the teaching reintroduce anything the curation policy removes?)*

A curated-out element can slip back in through a teacher's own example, board drawing, or off-plan aside even when the lesson plan was clean. Watch for:

| Watch | Curated standard (REF-01 §0) |
|---|---|
| Any **tune / singing / percussion** (incl. a sung rhyme) | Rhymes are delivered **spoken, not sung** (C-03/C-16). |
| **Board drawings** of a person/animal with a face | Faceless-figure rule — no eyes/nose/ears/mouth; prefer no living-being image (C-05). |
| **Mixed-gender pairing** in an activity beyond mahram/same-gender | Only mahram or same-gender groupings (C-02). |
| **Role-play / acting** as a character (never enacting prophets/sahaba) | Real practice as oneself is fine; pretending to be another is not (C-19). |
| **Western-default names/foods/clothing** slipped into examples | Replace every time; names from REF-20's matching class pool (C-10/F-01). |
| **Nationalist veneration** framing (salute, wreath, "great…", nation-over-ummah) | Teach the history plainly, ummah-priority (C-18). |
| **Creature/object agency** language ("the X saved/gave life/provided") | Reframe to "helped" / "survived by Allah's mercy" (C-09). |
| Mawlid/urs/grave/saint/bid'ah-as-norm framing | Replace (C-11–C-17). |

> **BREACH** = any curated-out element was reintroduced in delivery. Record exactly what and when. This is a coaching matter (the teacher likely did it without realising); repeated breaches after coaching are a curation-training need flagged to Project 07. *Mechanics questions* ("was this actually a breach?") route to REF-01/REF-21; genuinely unclear cases go to the Principal.

### G2 — Materials & Safety · উপকরণ ও নিরাপত্তা
*(applies the classroom-materials standard, D-049)*

| Watch | Standard (D-049) |
|---|---|
| What the **teacher** writes with | **Whiteboard + marker** (the school standard). |
| What **pupils** use | **খাতা / পেনসিল / কলম.** |
| Board legibility & environment | Board readable from the back; room safe, seating supports the activity. |

> **BREACH** = the teacher does not use the whiteboard + marker as standard, pupils lack খাতা/পেনসিল/কলম, or the environment is unsafe. The materials standard is school-wide (D-049); a breach is recorded regardless of lesson quality.

## §6 — Stepwise observer protocol (micro-management)

> **Observation method.** Classrooms are recorded continuously with **CCTV + sound**; the observer **reviews the footage** of a chosen session — no one sits in the room. This suits the rubric well: you can watch the full period, **pause and rewind** to verify the two gates, and several Leads can review the *same* recording to stay consistent. Its one limit: a camera cannot capture everything (an off-camera corner, an inaudible one-to-one word, board text too small to read), so **"not visible/audible on the footage" means *no evidence* — never a low score** (see step 7).

### 6.1 Before reviewing
1. **Pick the session to review.** Choose a normal, representative period — not a cherry-picked best or worst one. (A fair review needs an ordinary lesson.)
2. **Be transparent.** The teacher knows the room is recorded; tell them **which session you are reviewing** and that a **growth debrief will follow.** Because the room is *always* recorded, the difference between developmental review and mere surveillance is exactly this honesty and the debrief — review without feedback corrodes trust. Say plainly: *"This is a growth review to help, not a gotcha."*
3. **Collect the plan.** Get the **Chapter Plan + the specific Session Plan** for that period (REF-02). Read the session objective and must-cover slice so you know what *should* happen.
4. **Open the class's Bloom band** (§9) and have **REF-18** and the **§4.1 curation watch-list** to hand for the two technical checks.
5. **Take a blank §5 sheet.** One per session reviewed.
6. **Footage handling.** Use the recording only for this review, and store/retain/share it per the **School Handbook**'s recording policy (footage governance is an administrative matter, not REF-11's to set).

### 6.2 Reviewing the footage
7. **Watch the whole period, start to finish.** Partial review mis-scores structure (you miss the Opening or Exit-Check). Where the camera or audio cannot show something a domain needs, write **"not evident on footage"** and do **not** mark the domain down for it — judge only what the recording actually shows.
8. **Use pause and rewind for the two gates** — this is the advantage of footage over a live visit: re-watch the board to check for a drawn face, replay a segment to hear whether a rhyme was sung. Run both deliberately: any tune/face/mixed-gender/role-play/Western-default/veneration/agency slip (G1)? Materials standard met — whiteboard + marker, pupils with খাতা/পেনসিল/কলম, a safe room (G2)?
9. **Note evidence, not verdicts.** Write what you *see and hear* ("3 of 6 children answered the 'why' question"; "drew a face on the board") — the score comes after, from the anchors.
10. **If the footage shows a genuine child-safety concern, escalate it immediately** — to the Principal, outside this review — regardless of any scores. Child safety is never "averaged in."

### 6.3 Scoring (right after the review, before you forget)
11. **Score each domain from the anchors** (§3), not from a general impression. For each, write the **one concrete note** that justifies the level. Where a domain rested on something the footage couldn't capture, record the limitation rather than guessing.
12. **Mark both gates** PASS/BREACH; write up any breach **specifically** (what, and the timestamp).
13. **Decide the single growth focus** — pick the domain at 2 (or the gate breach, if any) that, if it moved to 3, would most improve the children's learning. One. (See §2.3.)

### 6.4 The debrief (soon after the review — see §7)
14. Hold the debrief privately, calmly, and soon. Follow the §7 protocol. Where it helps, **watch the relevant clip together** — footage makes feedback concrete and fair ("let's look at the moment your wait-time let three quieter children answer").
15. **File the sheet**, give the teacher a copy, and **log the growth focus** so the next review can check progress on it (§8).

## §7 — Feedback & debrief protocol

A score that is never discussed well changes nothing; a debrief done badly damages the teacher and the trust the whole cycle runs on. The debrief follows the school's own adab — honest, kind, and for the person's good (REF-12 §5–§8).

**The shape (keep to it):**
1. **Ask first.** Open with the teacher's own read: *"How do you feel that session went?"* Teachers often name their own growth point — which makes the rest easy.
2. **Name one genuine strength, specifically.** Not flattery — a real thing they did well, named precisely ("your wait-time after the 'why' question let three quieter children answer"). This is true *and* it sets the tone.
3. **If a gate breached, address it plainly and without drama.** State what you saw, why it crosses the curation/materials standard, and what to do instead. Treat it as a thing to correct, not a character flaw — most breaches are unknowing.
4. **Give the one growth focus.** One domain, one concrete next step the teacher can take next week. Make it achievable. Agree it together.
5. **Check progress on the last focus** (from the previous observation). Celebrate movement — *2 → 3 is a real win.*
6. **Close on confidence.** End with belief in the teacher, not a list of faults.

**Hard rules for the debrief:**
- **Never humiliate, never compare to other teachers, never raise it in front of pupils or colleagues.** Harsh feedback violates the very adab D4 measures.
- **Separate the teaching from the person.** "The exit-check was skipped" — not "you always forget."
- **One focus, not five.** (§2.3)
- **The rubric is the floor, not the ceiling** of the conversation — a good debrief is a coaching dialogue, not a reading-out of numbers.
- This is **not** a pay/discipline conversation (§1.3). Keep those worlds apart.

## §8 — How REF-11 feeds Project 07 and the trackers

REF-11 is the instrument; **Project 07 (Teacher Training & Review)** is where it lives in practice and where its findings become action. The intended cycle:

1. **Observe** (REF-11, this document) → score + one growth focus.
2. **Coach** — the growth focus becomes the teacher's personal development target.
3. **Aggregate** — recurring low domains *across teachers* reveal a **whole-staff training need** (e.g. if D2 sits at 2 school-wide, the next workshop is on Bloom's climb, drawing on REF-17). Recurring G1 breaches → a curation refresher (REF-01/21).
4. **Re-observe** — the next observation checks progress on the prior focus (the §5 "progress on last focus" line); the trend is what matters (§2.2).

**Tracker hook (Project 06).** The growth focus, the five domain levels, and the two gate results are the fields a simple **observation tracker** would hold so the *trend per teacher* and the *pattern across staff* are answerable. The tracker's exact fields and Bangla labels are **Project 06's** to design — REF-11 only specifies *what* is recorded (the §5 sheet), not the tracker schema.

**Cadence (suggested, Project 07 to confirm).** A light, regular rhythm beats rare high-stakes reviews — e.g. one footage review per teacher per month, plus self/peer review as practice. Frequency is a Project 07 operational decision, not locked here.

**Companions not yet built (sequencing, see header).** REF-11 was produced ahead of REF-09/10 (Question-Setting Guidelines) and the Project 07 training modules. When those land: D2/D5's assessment-related indicators can cite REF-09/10 directly, and §8's training cycle slots into the Project 07 workshop calendar. Folding those references is a **data-level supersede** (citations added), not a structural change — so it will not churn the instrument.

## §9 — Bloom band quick-reference by class (observer aid)

*Read-only echo of REF-18 §4 — the standard D2 is scored against. If this and REF-18 ever differ, **REF-18 (and REF-06 §3.6) govern** and this table is corrected.*

| 5 | Apply, Understand, Analyze | Evaluate, guided Create |

**Indicative % mix — chapter scope (REF-18 §4.2):**

| Class group | Remember | Understand | Apply | Analyze | Evaluate | Create |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class 1–2 | 35–45% | 30–40% | 15–25% | 0–10% | 0–5% | 0–5% |
| Class 3–5 | 20–30% | 25–35% | 25–35% | 10–20% | 0–10% | 0–10% |

> Bands are **per chapter**, not per session (D-050) — so in a single observed session you are judging whether the demand is *band-appropriate and climbing*, not whether one period hits the full mix. **Age rule both ways** (REF-18): don't under-demand older pupils (definition-only weakens them) or over-demand younger ones (keep language/context simple — a Class 1 child *can* evaluate: "Was it good that Ayesha helped her friend?
