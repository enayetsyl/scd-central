**3.1  Language Focus: Writing a Personal Letter**

> Follow the instructions given below when you write a letter to someone:
>
> 1. Write the address and the date at the top right side of the page.
> 2. Begin the letter using a greeting or salutation, like 'Dear ...'
> 3. Your letter should have three paragraphs:
>     - In the first paragraph, explain why you are writing.
>     - In the second, give some details. This is the body of the  letter.
>     - In the third, tell what you expect from the receiver or ask for a reply.
> 4. End the letter with—
>     - 'Love' or 'Lots of love' when you are writing to family or a close friend;
>     - 'With best wishes' when you know the person quite well;
>     - 'Yours sincerely' when you don't know the person very well.

## ⛔ উৎস-সীমা

1. ছাপা 41, activity 1.2: "Mamun wrote a letter to his friend Hasib describing about his garden." — "describing about" as printed. The letter that follows is framed as a reply about how he spends his free time, and the garden is its subject only in the middle paragraph.
2. ছাপা 42, letter: "Now a days, I have been busy in my vegetable garden." — printed as three words.
3. ছাপা 42, letter: "You will find various vegetables like- tomatoes, beans, cucumbers etc. in my garden." — a hyphen is printed after "like" where a colon or nothing would be expected.
4. ছাপা 42, letter: the opening paragraph says "I am glad to hear from you after so many days. You wanted to know how I am spending my free time." — this is a reply to an earlier letter, but activity 4.1 on ছাপা 45 then asks the pupil to write "a reply to Mamun's letter", and 4.2 calls the envelope "the envelope of Hasib's letter" while asking for Mamun's address on it. The direction of the correspondence changes across the unit without comment.
5. ছাপা 42: the letter body is set entirely in italic while the eight diagram labels are roman; no other reading text in the book is set this way.
6. ছাপা 44, activity 3.1, instruction 1: "Write the address and the date at the top right side of the page." This is correct for the model letter, but statement a) of activity 2.1 on the same page says "top left side" — the statement is one the pupils are asked to judge, so the contradiction is by design; recorded here only so that a later reader does not take a) for a printed rule.
7. ছাপা 44, activity 3.1: "This is the body of the  letter." — a double space is printed between "the" and "letter".
8. ছাপা 44, activity 3.1, instruction 2: "like 'Dear ...'" is printed with straight single quotes and closes with no full stop after the closing quote, while items 1, 3 and 4 end with full stops or colons.
9. ছাপা 44, activity 3.1, instruction 4: "End the letter with—" ends with a long dash. The glyph is reproduced as printed.
10. ছাপা 44, activity 3.1: the closings offered are 'Love', 'Lots of love', 'With best wishes' and 'Yours sincerely'. The model letter on ছাপা 42 uses "Your friend,", which is not among them, and activity 1.3 e) asks what language Mamun used in the closing section.
11. ছাপা 44: the 2.1 table gives an unruled empty column for the pupil's answer, with no heading such as "correct/incorrect" over it.
12. ছাপা 45, activity 4.2, envelope: the place name is printed **Ramgarh, Khagrachari**. This differs from **Ramgrah**, printed in the preposition box on ছাপা 8 (Unit 3, ⛔ 7). The two spellings of the same place name should be reconciled by a human editor; both are recorded as printed in their own files.
13. ছাপা 45, activity 4.2: "C/O" is printed with a solidus and no full stops.
14. ছাপা 43, activity 1.4: the notepaper carries one filled bullet and seven empty ones, so eight parts are expected. The labelled diagram on ছাপা 42 names exactly eight parts, but two of its labels ("First Paragraph", "Last Paragraph") overlap with "Body" as descriptions of the same three paragraphs, so the intended eight-item answer cannot be derived unambiguously from the diagram.
15. ছাপা 41, activity 1.1 picture d: a commercial email service's branded wordmark is reproduced in the illustration. Recorded because it is text carried only by artwork.
16. Section numbering runs 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 4.2 — groups 2 and 3 have a single member each. No number is skipped within a group.
