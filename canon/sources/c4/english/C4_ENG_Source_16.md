**3.1  Language Focus: Adjective**

> Look at the sentence– *Nepal is a beautiful country*.
> In this sentence, the word ‘beautiful’ describes the noun ‘Nepal’. Here, ‘beautiful’ is an adjective.
> An adjective is a describing word. It usually describes a noun or pronoun.
> Examples:
>
>     a. Kathmandu is a **big** city.
>     b. There are many **high** mountains in Nepal.
>     c. Nepal has **beautiful** temples.

### ছাপা 75 (PDF 76)

English for Today

**4.1  Language Focus: Making Question**

> In the conversation, Zarin and Biraj used the following expressions to ask questions to each other.
>
> - *Are you visiting Nepal?*
> - *Where are you from?*
>
> *Are you visiting Nepal?* – In replying to the question, you will say ‘Yes’ or ‘No’. This is a **Yes/No question.**
>
> **Examples:**
>
> - Is Nepal a small country?
> - Do you love travelling?
> - Is Mount Everest the highest mountain in the world?
>
> *Where are you from?* – In replying to the question, you cannot say ‘Yes’ or ‘No’, you need to answer by providing relevant information. This is a **Wh-question**. Usually, this type of question starts with words (what, where, when, why, who, which, how) and the answers of these questions are long.
>
> **Examples:**
>
> - What did your teacher say?
> - Where are you coming from?
> - When is your birthday?
> - Why do you like playing football?
> - Who do you play with?
> - Which food do you like to eat?
> - How old are you?

## ⛔ উৎস-সীমা

1. ছাপা 75, activity 4.1: the box quotes the conversation as using "*Where are you from?*", but the dialogue on ছাপা 71 prints "Where're you from?" with the contraction. The quotation silently expands what the source page prints.
2. ছাপা 73, activity 2.2 c): "What is the most popular festival in Nepal?" — the dialogue never says any festival is the most popular. Biraj says *Dashain* is **his favourite**. The question asks for something the text does not state.
3. ছাপা 71: "At the  airport, she met Biraj Thapa." — a double space is printed between "the" and "airport".
4. ছাপা 71: "There are 1310 mountains in Nepal." — the figure is presented as something Zarin's English teacher said. Read at maximum magnification as 1310; the claim itself is left exactly as printed.
5. ছাপা 71: "It’s the capital of Nepal, isn't it?" — the apostrophe in "It’s" is curly while the one in "isn't" on the same line is straight.
6. ছাপা 72: "Thanks for your suggestion." — Biraj has stated a fact about temples, not made a suggestion.
7. ছাপা 72: "We also fly kites, put on *tika* on our foreheads." — the two verb phrases are joined by a comma with no conjunction, and "put on … on" repeats the preposition.
8. ছাপা 72: "Good bye." printed as two words.
9. ছাপা 72, activity 2.1: the instruction says "Put tick (√) mark", using a radical sign for a tick and omitting the article before "tick". Nothing in the table is ticked.
10. ছাপা 74, activity 3.1: "Look at the sentence–" ends with a short dash before the example, and the full stop after "country" falls outside the italic run.
11. ছাপা 74, activity 3.1.1: the instruction sends the pupil to "activity 1.1" for adjectives, but 1.1 is the whole two-page conversation; six circles are provided with no indication of which six adjectives are wanted, and the dialogue contains more than six candidates (beautiful, highest, foreign, old, large, favourite, wonderful, nice).

13. ছাপা 75, activity 4.1: "the answers of these questions are long" — "answers of" as printed, and the claim that Wh-answers are long is stated without qualification.
14. ছাপা 75, activity 4.1: the bold on "Yes/No question." runs across the full stop; the parallel "Wh-question" a paragraph later does not.
