**3.1  Language Focus: Noun**

> ***Rahat*** and his ***family*** live in a small ***village*** in ***Rangpur.***
>
> In the above sentence, the words ***Rahat, family, village, Rangpur*** are nouns. A noun is a naming word. It is used for naming a person, an animal, a place, a thing and a group.
>
> **For example:**
>
> | Persons | Animals | Places | Things | Groups |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | Rahat, Sumi, Nikhil etc. | tiger, lion, cat, dog, pigeon etc. | Savar, Rajshahi, school, village etc. | book, pencil, bottle, shirt etc. | class, family, army etc. |

**3.2  Language Focus: Personal Pronoun**

> Rahat is nine years old. **He** is in class four. His younger sister is Rita. **She** is seven years old. **They** go to Kazipara Government Primary School.
>
> In the text, the words ***He, She, They*** refer to Rahat and Rita.
>
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | Rahat | he |
> | Rita | she |
> | Rahat and Rita | they |
>
> These words are personal pronouns. We use personal pronouns to replace words for people, places or things.
>
> | Personal Pronouns | |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Subject** | **Object** |
> | I | me |
> | you | you |
> | he | him |
> | she | her |
> | it | it |
> | we | us |
> | they | them |
>
> **Example:**  He asked ***me*** to collect books.
>     The cow give ***us*** milk.

## ⛔ উৎস-সীমা

1. ছাপা 31, activity 3.2: the second worked example reads "The cow give ***us*** milk." — subject-verb disagreement, printed inside the language-focus box that models correct usage.
2. ছাপা 29, reading text: the grandmother's age is given as a bare number, "She is 64.", while the father, mother and grandfather all get "years old". Inconsistent within one paragraph.
3. ছাপা 30, activity 2.1 b): "What is the name of Rahat's father? What does his father do?" — "his" can refer to Rahat or to the father just named; the sentence does not settle it.
4. ছাপা 30, activity 3.1: the "Places" column mixes proper and common nouns — "Savar, Rajshahi, school, village etc." — without comment, on a page that has just used capitalised proper nouns as its examples.
5. ছাপা 30, activity 3.1: the sample sentence is printed with the full stop inside the bold-italic run, "in ***Rangpur.***", unlike the other emphasised words.
6. ছাপা 31, activity 3.1.1: the instruction is "Circle the nouns", but three of the five sentences (a, c, e) also contain pronouns that the following activity 3.2 will teach; nothing tells the pupil to leave them alone. Item d) "I have a pet cat." opens with a pronoun.
7. ছাপা 31, activity 3.2: the box says "the words ***He, She, They*** refer to Rahat and Rita", but *They* refers to both children together, not to either; the three pronouns are not parallel in the way the sentence implies.
8. ছাপা 31, activity 3.2: the first table has no column headers, while the second carries a spanning header "Personal Pronouns" over **Subject** and **Object**.
9. ছাপা 32, activity 3.2.1 b): "the capital of  Bangladesh" — a double space is printed between "of" and "Bangladesh".
10. ছাপা 32, activity 3.2.1 c): the underline runs under "<u>the hen.</u>" including the full stop, while every other underlined string in the exercise stops at the last letter.
11. ছাপা 32, activity 3.2.1 b): the sentence to be rewritten replaces a place name with a pronoun, which the 3.2 box permits ("replace words for people, places or things"), but no worked example is given for a place, and the second sentence would need "It" where all four items otherwise concern people.
12. ছাপা 32, activity 4.1: the five clues mix noun phrases with direct questions — "Family members", "Place they live in", then "What do they do", "How do they help you" — and none is punctuated.
13. ছাপা 33, activity 5.1: the poem is printed with no author name, unlike the poem in Unit 14 which carries one.
14. ছাপা 33: the last two lines are identical, "A happy – happy family.", each printed with a short dash between the repeated word. The glyph is a short dash, not a hyphen; the exact character cannot be determined from the raster. Line breaks and the repetition are reproduced as printed and have not been collapsed.
15. ছাপা 33: the poem's lower lines are set over the top of the family illustration, and 5.2 ("Recite the rhyme in chorus.") calls it a rhyme where 5.1 calls the same text something to "recite"; the heading itself gives no genre label.
16. ছাপা 27, activity 1.1: the tree has nine portraits and nine label boxes, one of which is filled with "Me". The family described in the 1.3 text has seven named or counted members plus cousins, so the tree cannot be labelled from the reading passage; it is meant to be filled from the pupil's own family, which the instruction does not say.
17. Section numbering runs 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.1.1, 3.2, 3.2.1, 4.1, 5.1, 5.2. This is the first unit in the book to use three-level numbering.
18. The whole unit is raster only. No printed typographic detail below the resolution of a 200 DPI render — the dash in "A happy – happy family.", the full stops in "Mr." and "Mrs.", the extent of each underline — can be independently confirmed.
