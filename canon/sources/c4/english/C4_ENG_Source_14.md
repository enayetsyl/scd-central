## Tears of the Earth
**Abdullah Al-Mamun**

    Once the river was clear and bright,
    Now hides its tears from sunny daylight.
    The sky seems dim, the soil has sore,
    The trees can't dance just like before.

    The birds aren't seen near, the flowers get fade,
    In smoky air with heavy dark shade.
    The ocean cries loud because of pain,
    With a lot of waste instead of rain.

    But a small effort can make it right,
    That can give us a beautiful sight.
    Let's love our earth, both land and sea,
    And make it safe for you and me.

## ⛔ উৎস-সীমা

1. ছাপা 66, activity 3.1 and 4.1 together: the "Examples" box gives **pain – rain**, **right – sight**, **fade – shade** and **sea – me**. All four are end-rhyme pairs of the poem itself. Activity 4.1 on the same page then asks the pupil to find the poem's rhyming words, so four of the answers are printed above the question.
2. ছাপা 66, activity 4.1: the worked example is **clear – tear**. These two words rhyme with each other, but they are not an end-rhyme pair of the poem — "clear" ends line 1 and "tears" falls mid-line in line 2, whose end-word is "daylight". The poem's own six end-rhyme pairs are bright/daylight, sore/before, fade/shade, pain/rain, right/sight and sea/me. The exercise provides six slots, a) to f), with a) already filled by a pair from outside that set, so the six pairs cannot be listed in the space given.
3. ছাপা 64, activity 1.1: "Let's go for a walk in the nature around your school campus." — "in the nature" as printed. "Observe and take notes what you see." — as printed.
4. ছাপা 64, poem line 3: "The sky seems dim, the soil has sore," — "has sore" as printed.
5. ছাপা 64, poem line 5: "The birds aren't seen near, the flowers get fade," — "get fade" as printed.
6. ছাপা 64, poem lines 7–8: "The ocean cries loud because of pain, / With a lot of waste instead of rain." — as printed.
7. ছাপা 64: the poem is set over a full-page watercolour, so several lines run across mid-tone artwork and are low in contrast, particularly lines 5 to 8. Each was read at magnification; the readings should still be checked against the physical book.
8. ছাপা 65, activity 2.2: the gloss offered for **effort** is "trying hard to do something", a verb phrase for a noun; the gloss for **instead** is "choosing one thing over another", which describes a choice rather than the adverb's meaning.
9. ছাপা 65, activity 2.2: the one worked match is drawn as a physical arrow across the table from **hide** to "keeping something secret". The remaining five rows sit opposite meanings that are not their own, so a pupil reading the table row by row reads five wrong pairings.

12. ছাপা 66, activity 4.2: the instruction says "Write two actions that can make earth happy and sad", with "earth" lower case, while 4.3 in the next line prints "the Earth" capitalised twice.
13. ছাপা 66, activity 4.2: the two answer columns are headed only by the picture captions; nothing marks which column is for happy actions and which for sad, and both are lettered a) and b).
