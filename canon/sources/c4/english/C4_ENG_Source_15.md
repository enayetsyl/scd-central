Nusair and Samin are two friends. They go to Armanitola Government Primary School located in old Dhaka. Every morning, they walk to school along the river Buriganga.

On their way, they see people throwing trash and waste into the river. Dirty water from drains also flows into it. The river water looks black and smells bad. They know this water is polluted. It is not safe for the fish, the plants or for people. Nusair and Samin feel sad and wonder, "Why do people make the river so dirty?"

Environment Pollution

As they walk, many cars and buses pass by. They see black smoke coming from the vehicles. This smoke pollutes the air. Breathing this polluted air can make people sick. The vehicles also honk their horns very loudly. It hurts their ears. Very often, they cover their ears with their hands. They feel uneasy for this sound pollution. They ask each other, "Why are the vehicles so loud?"

**3.1  Language Focus: Present Continuous Tense.**

> Present Continuous Tense talks about actions that are happening now.
>
> We are **polluting** our environment. (The process of environment pollution is happening. That is why we can express it in present continuous tense.)
>
> **Examples**:
>
> | Subject | am/is/are | Verb+(ing) | Rest of the sentence |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | I | am | reading | a book. |
> | She/He | is | drawing | a picture. |
> | You/We/They | are | playing | outside. |

## ⛔ উৎস-সীমা

1. ছাপা 67, reading text: "They go to Armanitola Government Primary School located in old Dhaka." — "old Dhaka" with a lower-case *o*, where the district name is normally capitalised. The same unit capitalises Buriganga.
2. ছাপা 67, reading text: "It is not safe for the fish, the plants or for people." — the preposition is repeated before the third item only.
3. ছাপা 68, reading text: "They feel uneasy for this sound pollution." — "uneasy for" as printed.
4. ছাপা 67 and 68: the two quoted questions, "Why do people make the river so dirty?" and "Why are the vehicles so loud?", are printed with straight double quotation marks, while single quotation marks elsewhere in the book are curly. Recorded as printed.
5. ছাপা 68, reading text: "living a healthy life becomes impossible when people pollute the air, water, and make too much noise" — the list mixes two objects of *pollute* with a separate verb phrase, so "make too much noise" reads as a third thing polluted.
6. ছাপা 68, reading text: "The vehicles also honk their horns very loudly. It hurts their ears." — "It" refers back to the honking rather than to any noun in the previous sentence, and "their" changes referent between the two sentences, from the vehicles to the boys.
7. ছাপা 68, activity 2.1 b): "People should throw waste in the water." — a statement the pupil is to mark True or False; recorded because it is printed as an unmarked assertion in a book for nine-year-olds, with the answer nowhere given.
8. ছাপা 68, activity 2.1: the answer space is a short dotted leader with no column heading, and the leaders are set at three different indents down the five items.
9. ছাপা 69, activity 3.1: the heading "Language Focus: Present Continuous Tense." ends with a full stop, unlike the parallel headings in Units 9, 11 and 18.
10. ছাপা 69, activity 3.1: the box explains the target sentence in a parenthesis that restates it — "(The process of environment pollution is happening. That is why we can express it in present continuous tense.)" — and the example table's third column is headed "Verb+(ing)" with the suffix in brackets.
11. ছাপা 70, activity 3.1.1 b): "Some people are ............... (throw) **trashes** on the ground." — *trash* pluralised. Unit 9 on ছাপা 37 teaches uncountable nouns that "have no plural forms".
12. ছাপা 70, activity 3.1.1 c): "…loud noises by using horn." — no article before "horn".
13. ছাপা 70, activity 3.1.1: the exercise has four items where the parallel exercises in Units 9, 11 and 15's own 2.1 have five.
14. ছাপা 70, activity 4.2: "Make a poster on how you keep our environment safe.  Then present your poster in the class." — a double space is printed after "safe.", and the possessive shifts from "you" to "our" within the sentence.
15. ছাপা 67, activity 1.1: four pictures are given, but the reading text discusses three kinds of pollution — water, air and sound. Pictures a and b both show waste and water, c and d both show traffic and smoke; nothing in the picture set corresponds to sound pollution except by inference from the traffic.
