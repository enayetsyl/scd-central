## Executive Checklist — the 60-second teacher version

Use this before you open any lesson. The full rules are below; this is the at-a-glance.

1. **Scan** the lesson element-by-element against the 19 categories (C-01 to C-19, §4.1).
2. **Tag** each element: **KEEP-AS-IS**, **NEEDS-REPLACEMENT**, or **FLEXIBLE** (§3).
3. **When you replace, preserve four things** (§4.2): the learning outcome, the required vocabulary (word must appear somewhere, any position — §4.3), the Bloom's cognitive level (§4.4), and the reading-difficulty level.
4. **Images** — apply the faceless-figure rule (C-05) to any human or animal picture you keep: no eyes, nose, ears, or mouth. Prefer no living-being image at all.
5. **Mixed-gender** (C-02) — depict only mahram groupings (parents + children, siblings) and same-gender groups. Modify other mixed groupings to single-gender.
6. **Music & dance** (C-03, C-16) — remove. Convert sung rhymes to **plain spoken delivery** (read or said aloud) or spoken-rhythm syllable work — **not** to song or nasheed. Replace dance with a permissible action. All percussion counts as music. **Most often this is a single decoding word, not a song/dance theme** (একতারা, "গান গায়", "ময়ূর নাচে"): replace the word and keep the target letter/sound (§4.5 E-03).
7. **Western defaults** (C-10) — replace Western personal names (drawn from **REF-20**), foods, and clothing **every time** (no exceptions). Judge other Western framings case-by-case.
8. **Bid'ah / Sufi / grave / saint / mawlid content** (C-11 to C-17) — replace. For devotional poetry, **keep the poem form, replace the devotional content** with authentic Islamic content.
9. **Nationalist veneration** (C-18) — remove flag salutes, flowers/wreaths at the Shaheed Minar or other monuments, ritual silence, and any nation-over-ummah framing. Teach the factual history (Liberation War, Language Movement) plainly, reframed with ummah-priority. Also: **শহিদ in the nation sense** gets a spoken **teaching-note** correcting it to its Sharia meaning (§4.5 E-18b); **drop nation-glorifying qualifiers** ("মহান বিজয় দিবস" → "বিজয় দিবস") and **swap hero-portrait counting items for neutral objects** (§4.5 E-18c).
10. **Role-play** (C-19) — no pretending to be a fictional or other character (no acting/dramatization; never enact prophets or sahaba). Two pupils genuinely practising a real exchange as themselves (a real salaam, a real question) is fine — that is practice, not pretence.
11. **Creature agency** (C-09) — a creature or object that **saves a life, gives life, or provides** → reframe to "**helped**" / "**survived by Allah's mercy**" (§4.5 E-09b). A merely *talking* animal in a fable needs no edit.
12. **Can't decide a CASE-BY-CASE instance?** Escalate it to the Principal via §4.5. Do not guess.

## §1 Purpose, scope, and theological framing

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy is the school's operational rulebook for adapting NCTB textbook content to the school's Islamic commitments. It tells a teacher or content writer, without further interpretation, **what to replace, what to preserve, and how to handle the unclear cases**. It is consumed by lesson planning (Project 03), question banks (Project 04), and replacement-content writing (Project 05).

### 1.3 Theological framing (D-020 — Salafi methodology)

The school operates under the Salafi methodology: the Quran as the primary source, the authentic (sahih and hasan) Sunnah, and the understanding of the Salaf al-Salih (the first three generations) as the authoritative interpretive lens; positions are taken on the weight of evidence and the school is not bound to madhhab taqlid as a default.

Operational consequence: the replacement workload is **higher** than under a general Bangladeshi Sunni-Hanafi-Sufi cultural framing, because NCTB content reflects that wider cultural Islam — which includes practices the methodology treats as bid'ah (mawlid, urs and saint veneration, tawassul through the deceased, grave devotion, music in an Islamic frame, and devotional poetry carrying tariqah lineages). Plan replacement capacity accordingly.

### 1.4 Posture

- **Charitable, not polemical.** The school is charitable in attitude toward Muslims who hold differing practices; it simply does not platform those practices in its own curriculum. Replacement content corrects framing without disparaging people or other communities.
- **Preserve the skeleton, replace the flesh.** The learning the child is meant to gain is sacrosanct. Only the specific examples, stories, images, and framings change — never the outcome, the vocabulary, or the cognitive level (§4.2–§4.4). This is non-negotiable.
- **Surface, don't assume.** Where qualified Salafi scholars genuinely differ, the policy records the open question (§5) rather than inventing a stance. Niche fiqh that the school is unsure of is referred to a qualified scholar before being locked.

## §2 How to use this policy (teacher-facing workflow)

A teacher annotating a lesson works through these steps in order:

1. **Break the lesson into elements** — title, body text, each illustration, each exercise, any audio companion.
2. **For each element, ask: does it fall into any of C-01 to C-19?** Use the Definition and Examples fields (§4.1) to recognise the category. An element can trigger more than one category (e.g. a sung rhyme illustrated with children → C-03 + C-05).
3. **Assign a tag** (§3): KEEP-AS-IS, NEEDS-REPLACEMENT, or FLEXIBLE.
4. **If NEEDS-REPLACEMENT,** read that category's Replacement approach and note what must be preserved (§4.2: outcome, vocabulary, Bloom's level, reading level).
5. **If FLEXIBLE,** apply the modification the category specifies (often: keep the text, fix the image or the framing).
6. **Apply the global image rule (C-05)** to any retained living-being picture *after* all other edits.
7. **If a CASE-BY-CASE element won't resolve** (C-09, or the residual part of C-10), escalate it to the Principal via §4.5 rather than deciding alone.
8. **Hand the annotated lesson on** to replacement-content writing (Project 05) with the preserved-items note attached.

*Test of a good annotation:* a second teacher, reading only your tags and notes, could write compliant replacement content without re-reading the original lesson.

## §3 The three annotation tags

| Tag | Meaning | When to apply | Class 1 example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KEEP-AS-IS** | The element is compliant; use it unchanged. | No category is triggered; content and framing are already acceptable. | The *text* of a wholesome story such as *মায়ের ভালোবাসা* ("Mother's Love"); a letter-recognition drill. |
| **NEEDS-REPLACEMENT** | The element triggers a category with a REPLACE stance; it must be changed before use. | A category's default stance is REPLACE (or the element clearly violates the modification threshold). | A sung rhyme with melody (C-03); a Western personal name in a story (C-10); a milad-celebration scene (C-11); a flag-salute or monument-flower instruction (C-18); an "act it out as the shopkeeper" role-play task (C-19). |
| **FLEXIBLE** | The element is usable with modification; the learning value is fine but one aspect must be fixed. | The stance is PRESERVE-WITH-MODIFICATION, or only the image/framing (not the substance) is the problem. | A family illustration (keep the scene, render faceless per C-05); a mixed-gender classroom picture (modify to single-gender per C-02). |

**Mini decision tree.** For each element: *Is a category triggered?* → No → **KEEP-AS-IS**. → Yes → *Can the element be fixed by modifying the image/framing while keeping the substance?* → Yes → **FLEXIBLE**. → No → **NEEDS-REPLACEMENT**. *If you cannot tell whether a category is triggered (CASE-BY-CASE)* → apply the §4.5 escalation route.

## §4 Replacement rules

### §4.1 Replacement categories (C-01 to C-19)

Each category uses the 5-field template: **Definition · Examples in NCTB · Default stance · Replacement approach · Exceptions / edge cases.** Categories carrying explicit Principal direction are marked.

#### Core categories (C-01 to C-10)

**C-01 — Idolatry, polytheism, shirk-adjacent content**
- **Definition:** Content that depicts, names approvingly, or normalises the worship of — or the attribution of divine power or ultimate causation to — anything besides Allah (idols, deities, objects of devotion, rituals directed to other than Allah).
- **Examples in NCTB:** an idol/deity image presented as an object of devotion; a folk tale treating a non-Islamic deity as real and benevolent; festival illustration centred on idol worship. (Direct shirk content is uncommon in Class 1 literacy; it tends to enter through folk-tale or festival framings.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Re-anchor worship and gratitude in Allah alone, and ultimate causation in His decree; remove any idol/deity image and rebuild the lesson's outcome (a target word, letter, or concept) inside a permissible scene.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Factual, non-endorsing acknowledgement that other beliefs exist is a higher-class matter, not introduced at Class 1; if it arises, route to §4.5. Overlaps C-04 (festivals) and C-09 (causation framings).

**C-02 — Inappropriate mixed-gender depictions or interactions** *(Principal direction — Decision A)*
- **Definition:** Visual or narrative content showing non-mahram males and females together, or normalising cross-gender interaction beyond the school's modesty standard.
- **Examples in NCTB:** classroom illustrations mixing boys and girls; a named boy and named girl shown playing or interacting together; community/market scenes mixing non-mahram adults; the "me and my classmate" identity lesson (*আমি ও আমার সহপাঠী*).
- **Default stance:** PRESERVE-WITH-MODIFICATION (REPLACE where modification cannot carry the outcome). **Threshold:** depict **only mahram groupings (parents with children, siblings) and same-gender groups**; modify all other mixed groupings to single-gender.
- **Replacement approach:** Re-illustrate mixed scenes as same-gender groups or as a family/mahram scene, redistributing roles and dialogue so the vocabulary and sentence-pattern outcome is unchanged. Apply the C-05 faceless rule to any retained figure.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** A sibling or parent-child pair (mahram) is acceptable on gender grounds (still faceless per C-05) — see §4.5 E-02. Non-figurative representation (objects, faceless same-gender silhouettes) preferred where it carries the outcome.

**C-03 — Music and dance (including all percussion)** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q2)*
- **Definition:** Instrumental music of any kind (any sound produced by a musical instrument, including all percussion) and dance — as content, illustration, or audio companion.
- **Examples in NCTB:** rhyme/song units with melodic audio (Bangla *ছড়া* set to tunes; English Unit 5 "Rhymes and Sounds"); illustrations of children dancing or playing instruments; "dance" activity references.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE (at the music/dance component level).
- **Replacement approach:** Convert sung or melodic rhymes to **plain spoken delivery** — read or said aloud — or to spoken-rhythm syllable work used purely as a literacy device; the *text* of an acceptable rhyme may be KEEP-AS-IS while its musical setting is removed. **The replacement is spoken, not sung** (not a cappella, not nasheed — see C-16). Replace dance illustrations/instructions with a permissible movement activity that hits the same outcome.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** **No duff/daff exception** — all percussion is treated as music (duff/daff is also unavailable in Bangladesh; documented so the position stays explicit if it is ever introduced). Plain rhythmic clapping used purely as a literacy device (syllable beats) is not "music" and is acceptable. In Class 1 literacy the trigger most often appears as **single decoding words** (একতারা, তবলা, বাঁশি; "গান গায়"; "ময়ূর নাচে") and in illustrations rather than as song/dance themes — see §4.5 **E-03** for the standard rewrites. Overlaps C-16.

**C-04 — Festivals of other faiths (presented as one's own to celebrate)**
- **Definition:** Content presenting the religious festivals or devotional observances of other faiths as something the Muslim child celebrates, participates in, or treats as their own.
- **Examples in NCTB:** a lesson inviting children to celebrate another faith's religious festival; an illustration of the child taking part in another faith's devotional ritual; "our festivals" content blending Islamic and non-Islamic religious celebrations as equivalent. (Low incidence in Class 1 literacy; more likely in BGS / "Cultural Awareness" content — flagged forward.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Where a festival theme is needed for the outcome, anchor it in the child's own occasions (the two Eids); where the outcome is generic (counting, vocabulary), neutralise to a non-religious seasonal/community theme.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** National observances and nationalist content (independence day, language day, Liberation War commemorations, Shaheed Minar, flag/anthem ceremonies) are handled under **C-18**, not here. Factual, non-celebratory acknowledgement that other communities observe their own festivals is a higher-class matter, not introduced at Class 1. Overlaps C-01, C-10, C-18.

**C-05 — Depictions of living beings** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q1)*
- **Definition:** Photographs or drawings of complete living beings (humans and animals) shown with their natural facial features.
- **Examples in NCTB:** photographs and cartoon illustrations of children, families, and teachers throughout the books; character art for stories (Rubi, Tuli, Shubho; animal characters such as in *পিঁপড়ে ও ঘুঘু* "the ant and the dove" and *বাঘ ও রাখাল* "the tiger and the shepherd").
- **Default stance:** REPLACE (avoid depictions of living beings wherever possible).
- **Replacement approach:** First preference — carry the outcome with no living-being image at all (objects, scenery, letters, diagrams). Where a depiction is **genuinely necessary** for the outcome, fall back to PRESERVE-WITH-MODIFICATION using **figures without facial features** (no eyes, nose, ears, mouth), for humans and animals alike.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Inanimate objects, plants, scenery, and non-figurative diagrams are unaffected. C-05 is a **global rendering constraint applied after** any other category's content edit. The intra-Salafi difference on photographs vs hand-drawings is **resolved** (faceless figures; avoid where possible) and logged in DECISIONS — not reopened here. See §4.5 E-05.

**C-06 — Gambling, lotteries, games of chance**
- **Definition:** Content depicting or normalising betting, lotteries, raffles, prize draws, or any game in which money or value is risked on chance.
- **Examples in NCTB:** a story or exercise involving a lottery / "lucky draw"; prize-bond references (common in the Bangladeshi context). (Low incidence at Class 1 literacy.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Replace chance-based reward with an effort- or merit-based reward, or a gift, that carries the same outcome (e.g. a prize for completing a task; a present from a parent). Remove lottery/prize-bond specifics.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Ordinary children's games of *skill* (no money or chance staking) are acceptable. Prize-bond/lottery-savings content overlaps C-08 framing; under the C-08 "Narrow" stance, the chance/gambling aspect is handled here in C-06.

**C-07 — Alcohol, intoxicants, drug references**
- **Definition:** Content depicting, naming approvingly, or normalising alcohol, intoxicants, or recreational drugs.
- **Examples in NCTB:** a scene featuring wine/beer; an adult character drinking; vocabulary or illustration referencing an intoxicant. (Very low incidence at Class 1.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Replace the intoxicant with a permissible item (juice, water, milk, dates) that preserves the vocabulary or scene outcome.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Factual health education about the harms of intoxicants (non-normalising) is a higher-class matter, not Class 1. Overlaps C-10 (Western social settings where alcohol may appear incidentally).

**C-08 — Riba (interest) presented neutrally or positively** *(Principal direction — Decision B: Narrow)*
- **Definition:** Content that depicts or positively frames interest (riba) — a loan or deposit returning more than its principal because of the lending or time — including the word "interest" / *সুদ* explicitly.
- **Examples in NCTB:** a word-problem where lent money is repaid "with extra"; a savings lesson framed around "earning interest." **No such content surfaced in the Class 1 Bangla or English stability analysis — riba material is effectively absent at Class 1 literacy and would appear, if anywhere, in Math or higher classes. C-08 is scoped now for forward use.**
- **Default stance:** REPLACE the interest element (**narrow trigger**). **Neutral money, saving, and honest buying/selling content is ACCEPTABLE-AS-IS** — only an interest *return* is replaced.
- **Replacement approach:** Replace an interest example with a halal alternative that preserves the numeracy/vocabulary outcome — honest trade and profit (buying and selling), a gift, or qard hasan (an interest-free loan repaid in the same amount). Saving money in itself remains acceptable.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** **Contemporary-commerce questions** (conventional banking as the default, insurance, credit cards, prize-bond instruments) carry genuine intra-Salafi differences, are **out of scope for Class 1**, and are deferred to v2.0 — logged as the single OPEN entry in §5 (OPEN-01). Prize-bond/lottery content is handled under C-06.

**C-09 — Secular framings contradicting tawhid** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q3)*
- **Definition:** Framings that attribute ultimate power, causation, agency, or worship-worthy status to created things (nature, fate, luck, personified celestial bodies) in a way that displaces Allah as the cause and sustainer.
- **Examples in NCTB:** "the sun gives us light and life" stated as ultimate agent; nature personified as a willing actor; "good luck" / "fate" as a causal force; a rhyme thanking the sun or rain rather than Allah.
- **Default stance:** CASE-BY-CASE (annotator decides per instance using the criteria below; **no escalation default**).
- **Replacement approach:** Match the depth of edit to the severity. **Wholesale rewrite** when the text asserts causation/agency (e.g. "Allah gave the sun its light and warmth"). **Minimal or no edit** when it is harmless literary device with no belief claim (a talking animal in a fable, judged in context). Annotator criteria: (a) Does it assert that a created thing *causes or controls* outcomes? → edit. (b) Pure personification with no causal or devotional claim? → likely acceptable. (c) Does it invite gratitude or worship to other-than-Allah? → edit.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Instances the annotator cannot resolve are escalated via §4.5 (the **only** escalation route — there is no standing default). Overlaps C-01: C-01 is the harder image/worship cases, C-09 the softer framing cases. See §4.5 E-09. **Settled sub-pattern:** a creature or object that *saves a life, gives life, or provides rizq* is reframed by a fixed standard and is **not** re-escalated — see §4.5 **E-09b** (a merely *talking* animal making no causal claim remains no-edit).

**C-10 — Western or non-Islamic cultural framings presented as universal** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q4: Hybrid)*
- **Definition:** Content presenting Western or non-Islamic cultural defaults (names, foods, clothing, settings, idioms, holidays, narrative conventions) as the universal or normal frame for the child.
- **Examples in NCTB:** Western personal names in stories and exercises; Western foods as default examples; Western clothing as default dress; Western settings, idioms, holidays, or narrative conventions.
- **Default stance:** **Hybrid.** *Systematic REPLACE — no exceptions:* Western personal names, Western foods (as default examples), Western clothing (as default examples). *CASE-BY-CASE residual:* other Western framings (settings, idioms, holidays, narrative conventions) — annotator judgment.
- **Replacement approach:** Substitute Bengali-Muslim personal names drawn from **REF-20 (Approved Names Pool)**, using the matching class pool (e.g. Class 1 content → the C1 pool); substitute local/halal foods and modest local dress likewise — preserving the vocabulary and sentence-pattern outcome. (REF-20 is the single vetted source for name substitution; no substitute names are listed inline.) For residual framings, edit where the framing displaces the child's own context. **Propagation flag F-01:** the systematic exclusions must appear in Projects 03/04/05 reference cards; for personal names specifically, draw from REF-20's matching class pool.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** A foreign name or food that is the literal *subject* of a factual lesson (rare at Class 1) routes to §4.5 (E-10) rather than being auto-replaced. Residual CASE-BY-CASE deadlocks escalate via §4.5. Religious festivals are C-04, not C-10. Overlaps C-09.

#### Salafi-specific categories (C-11 to C-17)

**C-11 — Mawlid / milad celebrations presented as Islamic norm**
- **Definition:** Content presenting mawlid / milad un-Nabi (celebrating the Prophet's ﷺ birth) as a normative Islamic practice to observe.
- **Examples in NCTB / cultural content:** a lesson or illustration depicting a milad gathering or mawlid procession; framing milad as "our Islamic celebration." (More likely in BGS / Islamic-studies-adjacent content than Class 1 literacy — flagged forward.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Where an "Islamic occasion" theme is needed, anchor it in the established occasions (the two Eids, Jumu'ah, Ramadan at an age-appropriate level). Ruling content: the Salaf did not observe a birth celebration for the Prophet ﷺ; the school does not platform it as normative (this is the position of contemporary scholars including Bin Baz, Al-Albani, and Ibn Uthaymeen — attribution supplemental).
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Teaching the seerah and love of the Prophet ﷺ is encouraged and is **not** C-11 — C-11 is specifically the *celebration/observance* framing. Overlaps C-15.

**C-12 — Urs, saint veneration, shrine-centred devotion**
- **Definition:** Content presenting urs ceremonies, veneration of saints/pirs, or shrine (mazar)-centred devotion as religious practice.
- **Examples:** an illustration or story of mazar visitation for blessings; an urs gathering; devotion to a pir; seeking help at a shrine. (Low at Class 1 literacy; possible in regional/cultural content — flagged forward.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Re-anchor worship and supplication in Allah alone. If a "respected figure" theme is needed, use parents, teachers, and righteous people honoured *without* devotional or intercessory framing. Ruling content: vows, devotion, and seeking benefit or intercession at shrines or to saints are impermissible; worship is for Allah alone.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Ordinary, non-devotional respect for elders and scholars is acceptable and not C-12. Overlaps C-13, C-14.

**C-13 — Tawassul through deceased intermediaries**
- **Definition:** Content presenting the seeking of nearness to Allah, intercession, or fulfilment of needs *through* deceased persons (calling on them, asking them to intercede).
- **Examples:** a supplication addressed to a deceased saint or figure; a story where a need is met by appealing to a dead person's status; "ask [the deceased] to intercede for you" framing.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Re-anchor supplication directly to Allah. If a "drawing near to Allah" theme is needed, the permitted forms of tawassul may be used — through Allah's names and attributes, through one's own good deeds, or through the du'a of a *living* righteous person. Ruling content: invoking or seeking intercession from the deceased is prohibited; du'a is worship directed to Allah.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Asking a *living* person to make du'a for you is permissible and is not C-13. Scope is **deceased intermediaries** specifically. Overlaps C-12, C-14.

**C-14 — Grave-related practices beyond authentic ziyarah**
- **Definition:** Grave-related practices exceeding authentic ziyarah (visiting graves to remember death and to make du'a *for* the deceased) — e.g. du'a *at* or *to* graves for one's own needs, building over or decorating graves, circumambulating, offerings.
- **Examples:** an illustration of a decorated tomb as a devotional site; a scene of praying *at* a grave for one's needs; offerings left at a grave. (Low at Class 1 literacy.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** If a "remembering those who have passed" theme is needed, use authentic ziyarah framing — visiting to remember death and to make du'a *for* the deceased — without any devotional act directed at the grave. Ruling content: building over graves, taking them as places of worship, and seeking needs at them are prohibited.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Simple, dignified burial and remembrance content is acceptable. Overlaps C-12, C-13.

**C-15 — Bid'ah presented as normative Islamic practice** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q5)*
- **Definition:** Any innovated religious practice (bid'ah) presented as a normative or authentic part of Islam. **Explicitly includes Bengali devotional poetry carrying tariqah lineages** — devotional attribution to spiritual masters, lineage chains, or bid'ah elements presented as Islamic devotion.
- **Examples:** a rhyme or poem in praise of a pir or spiritual master as devotion; devotional verses invoking a tariqah lineage; an "Islamic practice" lesson teaching an innovated ritual as established Sunnah.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** **The form may be preserved; the content is replaced.** Keep the poem/rhyme format (and its literacy and vocabulary outcome) but substitute authentic Islamic poetry — praise of Allah, love of the Prophet ﷺ within authentic bounds, good character, the natural world as Allah's signs — for the tariqah-devotional content.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Culturally Bengali, non-devotional poetry with no tariqah or bid'ah content is judged on its general content, not under C-15. **Scope split with C-17:** poetry with a tariqah lineage → C-15; tariqah *orders/rituals/hierarchies* → C-17 (propagation flag F-02). See §4.5 E-15.

**C-16 — Music-based educational content framed as Islamic**
- **Definition:** Educational content set to music or melody and framed as Islamic (e.g. an Islamic-themed rhyme delivered as a song with instrumental or melodic backing).
- **Examples:** an "Islamic song" audio companion; a na'at or hamd delivered with instrumental music; alphabet or number content set to a melody and labelled Islamic.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE (the musical component).
- **Replacement approach:** Deliver the same Islamic content as **plain spoken word** — retain the acceptable text, remove the musical setting. Quran tilawah and du'a stay in their own proper recited forms and are never set to a melody. Consistent with C-03; C-16 is specifically the *Islamic-framed* subset.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** **Nasheed is not encouraged** — the replacement target form is plain spoken delivery, not song (whether instrumental or a cappella / unaccompanied). Overlaps C-03. *Basis: Principal direction, 2026-05-21.*

**C-17 — Sufi tariqah framings (orders, rituals, hierarchies)** *(Principal direction — README §14 Q5)*
- **Definition:** Content presenting specific Sufi tariqah orders, their devotional rituals (e.g. ecstatic dhikr forms outside the Sunnah), or their spiritual hierarchies (murshid–murid chains) as Islamic practice. **Scope is orders, rituals, and hierarchies — not poetry** (poetry with a tariqah lineage → C-15).
- **Examples:** a lesson naming or endorsing a tariqah order; a depiction of an innovated dhikr ritual or ecstatic ceremony; a murshid–murid hierarchy presented as the path to Allah.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE.
- **Replacement approach:** Replace with authentic worship and remembrance (dhikr in established forms) and with the believer's direct relationship to Allah and to legitimate teachers (without a devotional hierarchy). If a "spiritual development" theme is needed, anchor it in salah, the Quran, and good character.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** General good-character or spirituality content with no tariqah order, ritual, or hierarchy is not C-17. Scope split with C-15 (poetry); see §4.5 E-15 and flag F-02.

#### Categories added at v0.2 (field-test additions)

*These two categories were surfaced during Principal review of v0.1, via the additive-revision mechanism (numbering continues C-18 onward; existing codes unchanged).*

**C-18 — Nationalism over ummah; nationalist veneration rituals** *(Principal direction — Q1, 2026-05-21)*
- **Definition:** Content that (a) directs ceremonial veneration or reverence toward national symbols, monuments, or martyrs — flag salutes, standing for the anthem as an act of reverence, laying flowers or wreaths at the Shaheed Minar or similar monuments, ritual silence — or (b) frames national identity as taking precedence over the Muslim ummah.
- **Examples in NCTB:** the Liberation War lesson present in every edition (*মুক্তিযুদ্ধ* / *আমাদের মুক্তিযুদ্ধ*); national-day content; instructions to salute the flag, sing the anthem with reverence, or offer flowers at a monument; "our nation above all" framing.
- **Default stance:** REPLACE the veneration rituals and the nation-over-ummah framing. **Factual history is KEEP-AS-IS with modification:** the events (the Liberation War, the Language Movement) may be taught as plain historical fact, reframed so that Muslim-ummah identity is primary and no ceremonial reverence is directed at symbols or monuments.
- **Replacement approach:** Where a lesson commemorates through ritual, replace the ritual with sober factual remembrance — what happened, when, the human cost — with gratitude and reliance directed to Allah. Remove flag-salute, flower-offering, and ritual-silence instructions. Keep the literacy/vocabulary outcome (target words, reading comprehension) intact; only the veneration acts and the nation-over-ummah framing are replaced. Where affection for one's homeland appears, frame it within an Islamic frame that does not elevate nation over ummah.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Plain patriotism (affection for one's land) without ceremonial veneration or ummah-displacing framing is not the target of C-18. Factual history is retained per the rule above — see §4.5 E-18. Two further field-surfaced forms are handled at §4.5: the **creedal mislabel of শহিদ in the nation sense** (a spoken teaching-note fix) at **E-18b**, and **quieter veneration forms** (nation-glorifying qualifiers; hero-portrait counting items) at **E-18c** — both are *clear* fixes, not OPEN-02 borderline acts. Overlaps C-01 (reverence to other than Allah), C-09, C-10. *Borderline physical acts (e.g. standing for an anthem vs. saluting a flag) carry ruling nuance; where a precise ruling is needed, confirm with a qualified scholar before locking it (§12; §5 OPEN-02).* **Propagation flag F-03 (extended at v1.1):** high-frequency (the Liberation War lesson appears in every edition) and now also carries the E-18b teaching-note fix class and the E-18c forms — must appear in Projects 03/04/05 reference cards (the teaching-note class additionally touches Project 03 lesson plans and Project 07 training).

**C-19 — Role-play, dramatization, and impersonation** *(Principal direction — Q2, 2026-05-21)*
- **Definition:** Activities requiring a learner to *pretend to be* a fictional or other character — acting, dramatization, enacting a scene as someone they are not, impersonation. A **method-level** category (like C-05): it constrains the *activity type*, not a content theme.
- **Examples in NCTB:** "act out the story" dramatization tasks; "be the shopkeeper / be the doctor / be the tiger" role-play prompts; skit or play activities; enacting a character within a dialogue. (Common in the Era-3 English communicative units — Greetings and Farewells, Questions and Answers, Classroom Instructions.)
- **Default stance:** REPLACE the role-play activity. **Boundary:** *barred* = pretending to be a fictional or other character. *Allowed* = genuine practice of a real exchange the child would actually use (e.g. two pupils greeting each other with a real salaam, or asking and answering a real question) — that is real practice, not pretence.
- **Replacement approach:** Preserve the learning outcome — often a *speaking/communicative* outcome (§4.2) — by substituting genuine practice for pretence: pupils practise the real language **as themselves** (real greetings, real questions and answers, real classroom instructions) rather than enacting a character. Where NCTB used a dramatized scene to teach comprehension, substitute direct comprehension work (retell, answer, sequence) at the same Bloom's level.
- **Exceptions / edge cases:** Genuine real-exchange practice between pupils as themselves is acceptable (the allowed side of the boundary) — see §4.5 E-19. C-19 also covers **never enacting or impersonating the prophets or the sahaba** (independently impermissible). A real paired exchange must still respect the C-02 threshold — pair pupils same-gender. Overlaps C-02, C-05.

### §4.2 Replacement principles — what must be preserved

When content is replaced, four things are preserved without exception. Replacement changes the *flesh*; it never changes the *skeleton*.

1. **Learning outcome** — what NCTB intends the student to learn from the element (the literacy skill, concept, or competency). The replacement must deliver the same outcome.
2. **Required vocabulary** — the target words, especially the Universal Core and Stable tiers from the Class 1 stability analyses (see §4.3).
3. **Bloom's cognitive level** — the replacement must sit at the same cognitive level as the original (see §4.4).
4. **Reading-difficulty level** — appropriate to Class 1; the replacement must not raise or lower the reading demand.

### §4.3 Vocabulary preservation rules *(Principal direction — README §14 Q6: word-level, position-free)*

- **The required vocabulary word must appear somewhere in the replacement content. Its sentence position is not constrained.** A word that appeared in the third sentence of the original may appear in the first sentence of the replacement.
- **Corollary — learnable context.** The word must still be used in a real, sense-appropriate context that supports the child learning it. A token drop-in that does not teach the word does not satisfy the rule.
- **Source of the required-word list:** the Universal Core and Stable vocabulary tiers identified in the Class 1 Bangla and English stability analyses. (The transient/singleton tiers are not binding.)

### §4.4 Bloom's-level preservation

- The replacement must hit the **same cognitive level** as the original: recall stays recall; recognition stays recognition; an inference or application task stays inference or application.
- **Do not accidentally downgrade** (e.g. turning an "apply / explain" task into a "name / recall" task) or upgrade beyond the child's level when replacing the flesh.
- Cross-reference: *Bloom's Taxonomy Comprehensive Primer V1A* (Project 00) for the level definitions used here.

### §4.5 Edge cases (documented decisions)

Each entry is one documented decision. New edge cases are appended as field use surfaces them and are listed in category order. *(v1.1 added E-03, E-09b, E-18b, E-18c from the Class 1 Bangla skeleton pilot.)*

- **E-02 (C-02) — Sibling / parent-child pair illustration.** A mahram pairing (parents with children, siblings) is acceptable on gender grounds and need not be modified to single-gender; it remains subject to the C-05 faceless rule. *Basis:* Principal Decision A (2026-05-21).
- **E-03 (C-03) — Music/dance words and actions inside literacy practice.** At Class 1 the music/dance trigger most often appears not as a song or dance *theme* but as **single decoding words** (instrument and music/dance vocabulary — একতারা, তবলা, বাঁশি; "গান গায়"; "ময়ূর নাচে") and in **illustrations**. These are routine NEEDS-REPLACEMENT triggers. **Standard rewrites** (apply while preserving the target letter/sound — §4.3): a bird **ডাকে**, not গায়; a peacock **পেখম মেলে** / **হাঁটে**, not নাচে; replace an instrument noun with a **clean noun carrying the same target letter/sound**; "গান গায় / শোনে / শেখে" → **গল্প / ছড়া / কুরআন** (বলে / পড়ে / শোনে / শেখে). **Guardrail:** the instrument/music word being swapped is almost always a decoding *instance* (an example of the target letter), so the swap is free. If the word being removed is itself on the required-vocabulary list (Universal Core / Stable tier, §4.3) — rare for instrument names — it is **not** a free swap; preserve the word's letter/sound and escalate the conflict via E-09. *Basis:* Principal Decision (2026-05-26), DRR-2. Propagation flag F-05.
- **E-05 (C-05) — Faceless-figure rendering order.** The faceless rule is applied **after** any other category's content edit, to any retained human or animal image. Photographs are avoided; where a depiction is unavoidable, features are removed. The photographs-vs-drawings intra-Salafi difference is resolved (faceless figures) and logged in DECISIONS — not reopened in §5.
- **E-09 (C-09 / C-10 residual) — Annotator-deadlock escalation.** When an annotator cannot resolve a CASE-BY-CASE instance (C-09, or the residual part of C-10), the instance is escalated to the Principal here. This is the **only** escalation route; there is no standing escalation default.
- **E-09b (C-09) — Creature or object as agent of saving, life, or provision.** A story line attributing **saving a life, giving / creating life, or providing rizq** to a creature or object (e.g. "একটা পায়রা একটা প্রাণ বাঁচাল" — "a dove saved a life") touches tawhid: giving life (al-Muḥyī) and provision (ar-Razzāq) are Allah's alone, and ultimate causation is His. **Standard reframe — now settled; do not re-escalate this pattern:** → "**সাহায্য করল**" ("helped") or "**আল্লাহর রহমতে বেঁচে গেল**" ("survived by Allah's mercy"). **Why each part is settled:** *giving / creating life* and *providing rizq* by a creature are reframed as a matter of **creed** (these are divine attributes). *Saving* (rescue) describes a **means (sabab)** — and the methodology affirms real, Allah-created secondary causation (Allah is *Musabbib al-Asbāb*), so "a creature saved someone" parallels "the doctor saved him" and is **not itself shirk**; it is reframed here as a **precautionary, tawhid-language standard** for Class-1 learners, not because the phrase is impermissible. **Carve-out preserved:** a merely *talking* animal making no causal or devotional claim is **not** C-09 and needs no edit (see C-09 and Worked Example 3). **Does not reopen D-PROJ02-004:** C-09 remains CASE-BY-CASE / escalation-only for *genuinely ambiguous* causation; E-09b only removes this one recurring pattern from the escalation queue. *Basis:* Principal Decision (2026-05-26), DRR-4 (precautionary framing confirmed). Propagation flag F-06.
- **E-10 (C-10) — Foreign item as lesson subject.** A foreign name or food that is the literal *subject* of a factual lesson (rare at Class 1) is handled here rather than auto-replaced under the systematic rule.
- **E-15 (C-15 / C-17) — Poetry-vs-order scope split.** Devotional poetry carrying a tariqah lineage is C-15 (keep the poem form, replace the devotional content). Tariqah orders, rituals, and hierarchies are C-17. *Basis:* README §14 Q5; propagation flag F-02.
- **E-18 (C-18) — Factual history vs. veneration.** The Liberation War and Language Movement may be taught as plain historical fact, reframed with Muslim-ummah priority; the veneration rituals (flag salute, flowers at the Shaheed Minar, ritual silence) and the nation-over-ummah framing are removed. *Basis:* Principal Q1 (2026-05-21).
- **E-18b (C-18; §1.3 creed frame) — শহিদ used in the nationalist ("died for the nation") sense.** NCTB Class 1 Bangla (পাঠ ৫২) calls the Liberation-War dead **শহিদ** in the *nation* sense. Under §1.3 (D-020), shahādah is **fī sabīlillāh** — and, by authentic Sunnah, also for one killed **defending their life, family, property, or faith** (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, an-Nasa'i — graded sahih by al-Albani; with the further categories of plague, drowning, etc. in Bukhari / Muslim). Martyrdom and its reward are **conferred by Allah on the basis of intention** — not by a nation, and not earned by "dying for the country" as such; and we do **not** declare any specific named person a shaheed with certainty. **Ruling:** the equation **"died-for-the-country = শহিদ" is do-not-teach.** Where শহিদ appears in the nation sense and the surrounding lesson is retained, the teacher applies a **teaching note** correcting the word to its Sharia meaning — a **new class of fix** (the teacher corrects the word's meaning aloud), distinct from an image or framing edit. **Locked teaching-note content (read/conveyed by the teacher):** *"শহিদ in Islam means one killed fighting in the path of Allah — and, by authentic hadith, one killed defending their life, family, property, or faith. Martyrdom and its reward are from Allah, granted according to intention; they are not conferred by a nation, nor earned simply by dying for one's country. We do not declare any particular person to be a shaheed with certainty; we hope Allah accepts the righteous."* **Distinct from OPEN-02:** this is a *terminology* correction, not a borderline *physical-act* question — it is **not** escalated. *Basis:* Principal Decision (2026-05-26), DRR-1 (teaching-note wording locked as-is). Propagation flag F-03 (extended; introduces the teaching-note fix class → Projects 03 / 07).
- **E-18c (C-18) — Quieter veneration forms: glorifying qualifiers and hero-portrait items.** Two subtler forms beyond the overt rituals C-18 already lists (salute / wreath / silence). **(1) Glorifying qualifier:** drop glorifying superlatives applied to the **nation, national days, or national symbols** — "**মহান** বিজয় দিবস" → "বিজয় দিবস." *This targets the nation-glorifying usage only; it does **not** touch মহান applied to Allah (আল্লাহ মহান), which is correct and encouraged.* **(2) Hero-portrait / figure as example or counting item:** replace **real-person hero portraits or figure items used as examples or countable objects** (e.g. "সাত জন বীরশ্রেষ্ঠ" as the number-7 picture; পুতুল/doll counting items) with **neutral countable objects** — same number, same counting/vocabulary outcome — and apply the C-05 faceless rule to any figure that is retained. This satisfies C-18 (veneration via portrait) and C-05 (real-person faces) in one edit. **Not in the OPEN-02 gray zone:** E-18c fixes are *clear* framing/image edits — annotators apply them directly and do **not** escalate them. *Basis:* Principal Decision (2026-05-26), DRR-3. Propagation flag F-03 (extended).
- **E-19 (C-19) — Pretence vs. real practice.** Pretending to be a fictional or other character is barred; two pupils genuinely practising a real exchange as themselves (a real salaam, a real question) is acceptable practice. Pair pupils same-gender per C-02. *Basis:* Principal Q2 (2026-05-21).

## §6 Working examples (policy applied end-to-end)

Each example shows a real Class 1 lesson run through the workflow: categories triggered → tag → preserved items → replacement.

**Example 1 — Bangla story *মায়ের ভালোবাসা* ("Mother's Love")**
- *Triggered:* C-05 (illustrations of mother and child). C-02 **not** triggered — mother and child are mahram. Story theme is wholesome.
- *Tag:* KEEP-AS-IS (text) + FLEXIBLE (illustration).
- *Preserved:* reading-comprehension outcome; target vocabulary; Bloom's (comprehension); Class 1 reading level.
- *Replacement:* render the illustration as faceless figures (or, if the outcome allows, replace with a non-figurative scene); keep the text.

**Example 2 — English Unit 5 "Rhymes and Sounds" (a rhyme with a melody)**
- *Triggered:* C-03 (the melodic/sung delivery; any dance action). C-05 if figures are illustrated. (C-16 would apply only if the rhyme were framed as Islamic — here it is a secular phonics rhyme, so C-03.)
- *Tag:* NEEDS-REPLACEMENT (the audio/music component) + KEEP-AS-IS (rhyme text, if its content is acceptable).
- *Preserved:* phonological-awareness outcome (rhyme and sound); the target sounds/letters; Bloom's (recognition/recall); the rhyme's vocabulary.
- *Replacement:* deliver the rhyme as **plain spoken word** (read or said aloud) or spoken-rhythm syllable work — not as a song or nasheed; replace any dance instruction with a permissible movement activity; faceless figures in any illustration. *(At Class 1 the trigger is more often a single decoding word — "গান গায়", "নাচে", an instrument noun — than a full melody; apply the §4.5 E-03 standard rewrites and keep the target letter/sound.)*

**Example 3 — Bangla fable *বাঘ ও রাখাল* ("The Tiger and the Shepherd")**
- *Triggered:* C-05 (tiger and shepherd-boy figures). C-09 to consider (a talking animal). C-02 not triggered (a single shepherd boy).
- *Tag:* FLEXIBLE — resolving to KEEP-AS-IS (text) + FLEXIBLE (illustration, C-05). C-09: the talking animal is a literary device making **no** causal or devotional claim → minimal/no edit.
- *Preserved:* narrative-comprehension and sequencing outcome; target vocabulary; Bloom's (comprehension); reading level.
- *Replacement:* faceless rendering of the tiger and the boy; text retained; no tawhid rewrite required. *(Had the line instead made the animal the agent that **saved a life, gave life, or provided** — as in the dove-and-ant fable — the §4.5 E-09b reframe would apply: "সাহায্য করল" / "আল্লাহর রহমতে বেঁচে গেল.")*

**Example 4 — English "At Village Home" (Unit 4 Lesson 4, 2026 edition)**
- *Triggered:* C-05 (figures in the home scene). C-02 (a family scene — mahram, so acceptable on gender grounds; if the illustration shows mixed non-mahram adults, modify to single-gender). C-10 residual to consider (is "home" framed in a non-local/Western way? — "Village Home" is already a local framing, so likely acceptable; judge the illustration). If a named child appears, the name is drawn from **REF-20** (C-10 systematic rule).
- *Tag:* KEEP-AS-IS (text) + FLEXIBLE (illustration, C-05; C-02 family unit acceptable).
- *Preserved:* home/family vocabulary; the lesson's sentence patterns; Bloom's; reading level.
- *Replacement:* faceless family figures in a modest local home setting; keep the text.

**Example 5 — Bangla Liberation War lesson (*আমাদের মুক্তিযুদ্ধ*, present in every edition)**
- *Triggered:* C-18 (nationalist commemoration — any flag-salute, monument-flower, or ritual-silence instruction, and any nation-over-ummah framing). C-05 if figures are illustrated. *(Watch also for the quieter C-18 forms and the শহিদ mislabel — see below.)*
- *Tag:* C-18 → KEEP-AS-IS (the factual history) + NEEDS-REPLACEMENT (the veneration rituals and the nation-over-ummah framing).
- *Preserved:* the historical facts and the target vocabulary; reading-comprehension outcome; Bloom's; reading level.
- *Replacement:* teach plainly what happened, with gratitude and reliance on Allah and an ummah-priority framing; remove any flag-salute, flower-offering, or ritual-silence instruction; faceless figures in any illustration. **Where the dead are called শহিদ in the nation sense,** apply the §4.5 E-18b spoken **teaching-note** (correct the word to its Sharia meaning). **Drop nation-glorifying qualifiers** ("মহান বিজয় দিবস" → "বিজয় দিবস") and **replace any hero-portrait counting item** (e.g. "সাত জন বীরশ্রেষ্ঠ") with **neutral countable objects** per §4.5 E-18c.

**Example 6 — English Unit 1 "Greetings and Farewells" (an "act out greeting your friend" role-play task)**
- *Triggered:* C-19 (the "act it out as a character" framing). C-02 (a paired exchange — pair pupils same-gender). C-05 if figures are illustrated. C-10 if the greeting form is Western, or if a named character is used (name from **REF-20**).
- *Tag:* FLEXIBLE → NEEDS-REPLACEMENT of the role-play framing, retaining the speaking outcome.
- *Preserved:* the communicative/speaking outcome (greeting and responding); target phrases and vocabulary; Bloom's (apply); speaking level.
- *Replacement:* pupils practise the real greeting **as themselves** (a real salaam and reply) rather than acting a character; pair them same-gender (C-02); use the real Islamic/local greeting where the original was Western (C-10).

*(These six examples cover the most common Class 1 cases — wholesome text with non-compliant images, music, fables, home/family scenes, nationalist commemoration, and communicative role-play. A worked example involving riba/gambling is deliberately omitted: no such content exists at Class 1, per §4.1 C-06/C-08.)*
